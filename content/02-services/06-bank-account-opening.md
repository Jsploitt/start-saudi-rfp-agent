# Bank account opening

**Outside the 15 working days. Tracked separately. The real bottleneck between "registered" and "operating" — and the step most likely to break a client's plan.**

---

## What it is

**[VERBATIM]** — homepage, step 6: *"GM visa and bank account, tracked separately."*

**[VERBATIM — blog]**:

> "Yes, a foreign-owned company can open a business bank account in Saudi Arabia once its CR is active."

**[SOURCED]** The bank runs its own compliance and onboarding, entirely outside the registration process and outside any provider's control.

## What Start Saudi does

**[SOURCED]** Tracks it. The homepage says so and does not claim more.

**[SOURCED]** Also relevant, from the FAQ **[VERBATIM]**:

> "Opening the bank account after incorporation may need a visit depending on the bank, and we tell you upfront."

**[SOURCED — Taajeel's Havenstone proposal, June 2026.]** Taajeel sells **"Open bank account"** as a named optional service at **SAR 4,500 per bank**. So real support is offered and it is priced per bank — which implies running more than one application in parallel is a normal and expected thing to do.

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** Per-bank pricing is itself useful information for a client: it says that applications are made to specific named banks rather than to "the banking system", and that hedging across two is a purchasable option. For a client whose launch date depends on the account — which is most of them — that is worth raising.

## What the client provides

**[SOURCED]** Banks typically require:

- The **CR** (must be active)
- The **MISA licence**
- **Articles of association**
- **National Address**
- **ZATCA registration**
- **Signatory ID**

**[SOURCED]** And where there is a foreign parent: incorporation documents and board resolutions — attested, as always.

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** Note the dependency chain: the bank needs the National Address and the ZATCA registration, both of which come from step 5. So the bank account cannot begin until the entire 15-day path is finished. It is strictly sequential, not parallel.

## How long it takes

**[SOURCED]** **2–3 weeks**, typically, after the CR is active.

**[SOURCED]** And the blunt version, from the delays list:

> Banks run compliance checks entirely outside registration and can take as long as registration itself.

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** So a realistic "decision to operating" for a foreign founder is the attestation phase, plus three calendar weeks of registration, plus two to three weeks of banking — before any question of the GM visa. This is the arithmetic that matters to the client and it is nowhere on the website. Putting it in a proposal is the highest-value thing a proposal can do for a first-time entrant.

## What it depends on

- An **active** CR — not just issued
- National Address and ZATCA registration complete
- The bank's own compliance appetite for the client's jurisdiction, activity and ownership structure
- **[SOURCED]** Possibly a physical visit, bank-dependent
- **[SOURCED]** For corporate shareholders, the attested parent documents — **[SOURCED]** described as the largest source of delay at this step

## What commonly goes wrong

**[SOURCED]** and **[EXTRAPOLATED]**:

- **Parent company documentation.** **[SOURCED]** Identified as *"the largest delay"* at this stage — apostille or embassy attestation handled outside Saudi Arabia on the client's own timeline, all over again, for the bank's benefit
- **The client assumed a bank account came with the company.** It does not, and the belief is common
- **A visit is required and the client is not in the country** — the one part of the process that genuinely may need travel, after a pitch built on not needing to travel
- **Compliance declines or extended review** for certain jurisdictions, ownership structures or activities
- **Salary and supplier commitments made against a launch date** that assumed banking on day 15

## Proposal notes

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** This is the section where a proposal earns its credibility, because it is where a competitor's proposal will be vaguest.

- **Give the 2–3 weeks explicitly, and say it starts after day 15**, not at day 15.
- **Reproduce the "may need a visit" caveat.** The site already says it *"upfront"*; a proposal that omits it after the website includes it is a straightforward breach of the voice.
- **Warn about the parent documentation round two.** A client who attested documents for MISA assumes they are done. They are not — the bank will want its own set.
- **Tell them what they can do before the CR**: identify the bank, understand its requirements, get parent documents attested in duplicate while the registration runs. That is the whole path being run.
- **If a launch date is in the RFP, test it against this step first.** It is almost always the constraint, and it is almost always the ambiguity that needs surfacing.
- **Do not promise an outcome here.** The bank decides. **[VERBATIM — blog]** the governing principle is that *"the pace of each government step is outside any provider's control"* — and a bank is less controllable still.
