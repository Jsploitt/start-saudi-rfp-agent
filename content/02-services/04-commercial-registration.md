## The distinction that matters most

**[SOURCED]** Licence versus registration, in the order they occur:

| | MISA licence | Commercial registration |
|---|---|---|
| Issued by | Ministry of Investment | Ministry of Commerce |
| What it is | Permission for a **foreign investor to own** a business | Registration of the business as a **legal entity** |
| Order | First | Second |
| Needed by | Any foreign-owned company | Every company |
| Lets you… | Own | Bank, contract, invoice, hire |

**[VERBATIM — blog]**:

> "A MISA licence permits foreign ownership in principle, but you cannot open a bank account, sign contracts, invoice clients, or hire staff until your Commercial Registration is issued."

**[SOURCED]** Saudi and GCC nationals forming a wholly Saudi- or GCC-owned company need only the second.

## What commonly goes wrong

**[EXTRAPOLATED]**, following from the dependency structure:

- **Inconsistency propagating from earlier steps** — a name or shareholding that does not match sends the filing back, and at this point the client believes they are finished
- **The client announces launch on CR issuance.** The CR is not trading capability: there is no bank account yet, and depending on activity there may be further licences
- **Activity codes on the CR turn out narrower than the business needs**, discovered when a contract falls outside them
- **The commercial registration attracts an annual renewal, alongside the Chamber of Commerce subscription.** *(General knowledge, not stated in any Start Saudi source — the blog confirms only that Chamber membership is paid annually to maintain active status. Confirm before putting renewal obligations in a proposal.)* A client who thinks of the CR as a one-off registration is surprised a year later — which is precisely the *"Is something up for renewal that nobody told me about?"* anxiety the homepage quotes.

## Proposal notes

**[EXTRAPOLATED]**
- **"The CR is issued. Your company legally exists."** Use it. It is the emotional peak of the whole path and the brand wrote the line already.
- **Immediately follow it with what the CR does not do.** The registered-versus-operating distinction belongs here, in the same breath, not in a later caveats section. This is the exact shape of the brand's signature move: state the win, name its boundary, do not apologise.
- **Give the client a "day 15" picture**: what exists, what they can do, what they still cannot do, and what happens next. That single paragraph resolves more timeline disputes than any amount of scheduling detail.
- **Mention the annual renewal** even though it is outside scope. Not mentioning it is the thing the brand's own copy complains about.
