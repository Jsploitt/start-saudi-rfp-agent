# GM visa and residency

**Outside the 15 working days. Tracked separately. The step that turns "I own a Saudi company" into "I can run it from here" — and the one clients conflate most.**

---

## What it is

**[VERBATIM]** — homepage, step 6: *"GM visa and bank account, tracked separately."*

**[SOURCED]** The blog's central point is that the phrase "investor visa" collapses **three different products**:

| | What it is | Attached to |
|---|---|---|
| **GM visa** | A work permit for the company's registered **General Manager** | The company |
| **Routine investor iqama** | Residency for partners/owners on a MISA-licensed registration | The company |
| **Formal investor residency** | A high-bar track within Premium Residency — minimum capital investment, minimum jobs created within a defined window, plus an application fee | The individual |

And separately, **Premium Residency**, which **[VERBATIM — blog]** is *"personal: it is not attached to an employer or to a specific commercial registration."* **[SOURCED]** It comprises financial tracks and category-based options. **[TO CONFIRM: the category names.]** The specific categories are not enumerated in any source available to this kit, and — as with the investor-residency thresholds below — they are revised periodically. Do not list them in a proposal from memory.

## The two facts clients most need

**[VERBATIM — blog]**:

> "The GM does not have to be a hired manager. If you own the company, you can list yourself as GM."

**[SOURCED]** And:

> Foreign owners need not obtain personal iqamas. You can appoint another resident as GM or become the resident GM yourself — residency and ownership remain legally separate questions.

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** Together these define the real decision, and it is a decision most clients have not realised they are making:

- **Own without residing** — appoint a GM who is already resident in the Kingdom. No visa process for the founder. Requires finding and trusting someone.
- **Own and reside** — be your own GM. Requires the GM visa, medical checks, biometrics, embassy attestation and a move.

For a solo foreign founder the second is usually the answer, and it is a life decision sitting downstream of an incorporation decision. **[EXTRAPOLATED]** Surfacing it early — at the articles stage, where the GM is named — is one of the highest-value things a proposal can do. See `02-services/03-articles-of-association.md`.

## What Start Saudi does

**[SOURCED]** Tracks it, separately from registration. **[SOURCED]** The process runs through **Qiwa**, the labour platform activated in step 5.

### The path, as Taajeel documents it

**[SOURCED — Taajeel's Havenstone proposal, June 2026. This closes Q33 and most of Q16.]** Taajeel sells the GM residency alongside company formation and labels the path **"15 Steps"** — though the slide actually prints **sixteen bullets**, separating the Qiwa work permit from receipt of the Iqama. The discrepancy is Taajeel's, not this kit's. The bullets as printed:

1. Submit the support letter with MISA
2. Get approval from MISA
3. Start the procedures for the employee's visa with HRSD
4. Get approval from HRSD
5. Issue a proof of work visa
6. Assistance with the Qualification Verification Program
7. Delegate a manpower office in the employee's country
8. Sign the work contract, stamped from "MOF" *(the source's abbreviation; in context it reads as the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, but it is not spelled out — confirm before using it in a proposal)*
9. Issue the resident visa from the Saudi Embassy
10. Stamp the working visa on the passport
11. Employee arrives in KSA
12. Medical test examination
13. Pay the Ministry of Interior fees
14. Issue medical insurance
15. Issue a work permit from Qiwa
16. Receive the **Residence Iqama** (Resident Identity)

**Durations, from Taajeel's own Time Frame slide:** support letter 2 days · MISA approval 2 · HRSD procedures 3 · HRSD approval 1 · work visa issuance **15** · manpower office delegation 1 · contract signing 1 · resident visa issuance **15** · employee arrival 3 · Ministry of Interior fees 1 · work permit from Qiwa 3. Three milestones — the medical test, the medical insurance and receipt of the Iqama — carry no duration on the chart.

**Total: about 47 days**, dominated by two 15-day blocks — the work visa and the embassy's resident visa issuance.

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** So the honest headline for a client is **roughly six to eight weeks after the CR**, heavily dependent on the individual's own availability for medicals, contract attestation and the embassy appointment. Step 6, the Qualification Verification Program, is worth flagging separately: it verifies the individual's professional credentials and it is the step most likely to surprise a client who assumed a work permit was administrative.

**Government fees: SAR 14,750 per person per year** — SAR 2,000 visa, SAR 9,600 HRSD work permit, SAR 650 Ministry of Interior, SAR 1,000 medical test, SAR 1,500 medical insurance. See `05-pricing.md`.

**The alternative Taajeel offers:** a **Temporary General Manager**, priced at SAR 8,000 for three months and SAR 4,000 per month thereafter. **[EXTRAPOLATED]** This is the answer to the gap between "the articles must name a GM at step 3" and "your GM's residency takes another six to eight weeks", and it is not mentioned anywhere on the Start Saudi site. For a solo founder or a small company with nobody resident, it is often the thing that makes the timeline work.

## What the client provides

**[SOURCED]**
- The identity of the registered General Manager
- The GM must become a **Saudi resident**
- **Medical checks**
- **Biometrics**
- **Embassy attestation**

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** Each of those is a personal, in-person process in the GM's home country or in the Kingdom, on the GM's own timeline — the same structural problem as document attestation, and equally outside anyone's control.

## How long it takes

**Roughly six to eight weeks after the CR** — see "The path, as Taajeel documents it" above, where Taajeel's own chart totals about 47 days across the fifteen milestones.

Three caveats that must travel with that figure. It is **one engagement's estimate**, from the Havenstone proposal, not a published Start Saudi figure. It is **calendar days**, not working days. And much of it sits on the individual's own diary rather than anyone's process — the medical examination, the contract attestation and the embassy appointment all move at the speed of the person, not the adviser.

Start Saudi publishes **no figure at all** for this, and the site says only that it runs on a separate timeline from the fifteen working days. A proposal should give the six-to-eight-week range with its provenance attached rather than presenting it as a promise. See OPEN-QUESTIONS Q16.

## What it depends on

- CR issued and Qiwa establishment file active
- The GM being named in the articles
- Saudi nationalisation quota rules at the point of hiring, if the GM is an employee rather than the owner *(the Saudisation / Nitaqat framework — general knowledge, named in no source available to this kit; confirm before it appears in a proposal)*
- The GM's own availability for medicals, biometrics and attestation

## Formal investor residency — the thresholds

**[SOURCED]** Requires *"a minimum capital investment and a minimum number of jobs created, typically within a defined time window, plus an application fee."*

**[SOURCED]** The blog is explicit that these thresholds **are revised periodically** and should be confirmed with MISA or a licensed adviser.

**Do not state a threshold figure in a proposal.** The source that would justify it says it changes.

## What commonly goes wrong

**[EXTRAPOLATED]**
- **"Investor visa" asked for, and nobody establishes which of the three products is meant.** This is the blog's own framing and it is the most common failure here.
- **The GM decision deferred** to the articles stage, then made hastily, then regretted
- **The client assumes residency comes with the company.** It does not; they are separate questions
- **A hired local GM is planned but not yet recruited** — a hiring dependency sitting on the critical path to operating, outside the adviser's control entirely
- **Medical or attestation scheduling** in the GM's home country adding weeks nobody budgeted
- **Premium Residency assumed to be a business route.** It is personal and detached from the company

## Proposal notes

**[EXTRAPOLATED]**
- **Name which of the three products is in scope**, in those words. Refusing to use "investor visa" loosely is a small act of precision that demonstrates competence immediately.
- **Put the own-versus-reside decision in front of the client early**, with its consequences. It is the "you don't know what you don't know" moment of this whole service.
- **Do not fold the GM visa into the headline timeline.** The brand carves it out; a proposal that quietly includes it to look faster has broken the promise it is built on.
- **If an RFP is ambiguous about whether the GM visa is in scope, that ambiguity must be surfaced, not assumed away.** It is a materially different engagement with a materially different price and timeline — which is exactly why it is one of the deliberate ambiguities in `proposal/sample-rfp.md`.
