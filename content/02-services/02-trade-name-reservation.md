# Trade name reservation

**Step 2 of 5. 48 hours. Runs in parallel with the MISA licence — which is the whole point of it.**

---

## What it is

**[VERBATIM]** — homepage: *"Your company name, reserved with the Ministry of Commerce."*

**[SOURCED]** The company's legal name, reserved so it cannot be taken while the rest of the incorporation proceeds. The reservation is what the articles of association and the commercial registration are then drafted against.

## What Start Saudi does

**[SOURCED]**
- Checks name availability against the Ministry of Commerce register
- Files the reservation
- **Files it at the same time as the MISA application, not after it.** This is the step that makes the 15 days work — **[VERBATIM — blog]**: *"Running the MISA licence review and the trade name reservation at the same time, instead of waiting for one to finish before starting the other, is what compresses the front half of the process."*

**[TO CONFIRM: whether name advisory is part of the service.]** Whether Taajeel advises on Arabic naming conventions, transliteration, or which names are likely to be refused, versus simply filing what the client supplies, is not published. It matters, because name refusal is a common and entirely avoidable delay. See OPEN-QUESTIONS Q31.

## What the client provides

**[SOURCED]**
- The proposed company name, ideally with alternates
- **[EXTRAPOLATED]** Arabic transliteration or an Arabic form of the name, where required

## How long it takes

**[VERBATIM]** — homepage: **48 hours**, in parallel with step 1. **[VERBATIM — blog]** The blog describes it as *"usually quick"*.

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** Because it runs inside the MISA licence's 10 working days, it consumes **no time on the critical path**. Its 48 hours never shows up in the total — which is exactly why it is worth explaining in a proposal rather than listing. A client comparing providers will otherwise add 2 days + 10 days and conclude the 15 is arithmetic sleight of hand.

## What it depends on

- The name being available and acceptable to the Ministry of Commerce
- **[EXTRAPOLATED]** Consistency with the name used across the attested shareholder documents and the articles — a mismatch here propagates

## What commonly goes wrong

**[SOURCED]** The blog names **"trade name clashes"** among the causes that send steps *"back into queues"*.

**[EXTRAPOLATED]**
- **The preferred name is taken**, and no alternate was supplied — a day lost going back to the client for a decision, on a step that is meant to cost nothing
- **The name does not meet Ministry naming rules.** *(What those rules restrict — names implying government affiliation, certain religious or national terms, names requiring a specific licence — is general knowledge, not stated in any Start Saudi source. Do not list specific restrictions in a proposal without confirming them.)*
- **Transliteration inconsistency** between the Latin and Arabic forms across documents
- **The trading name the client actually wants differs from the legal entity name**, and nobody said so until after the CR

## Proposal notes

**[EXTRAPOLATED]**
- **Ask for three names, in order of preference, in the proposal itself.** It costs the client nothing, it removes the most common cause of delay on this step, and it is a small visible demonstration of running the whole path.
- **Explain the parallelism where the six-step table appears.** The reader will do the arithmetic; answer it before they ask.
- Do not present 48 hours as a selling point. It is not a differentiator — it is a step that is free because of how it is sequenced.
