# The process — the 15-working-day path

The spine of this file is the homepage section **[VERBATIM]** *"The whole path, and how long each part takes"*, extended with the blog post *How Long Does It Really Take to Register a Company in Saudi Arabia?*

This is the single most reusable piece of content in the kit. It is the brand's proof, its differentiator and its structure all at once — and a proposal that reproduces it well has already done most of its job.

---

## The path as published

**[VERBATIM]** — homepage, with the timeline axis marked Day 0 · Day 5 · Day 10 · 15 working days:

| # | Step | Duration | Notes as published |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Investment ministry licence** | **10 working days** | "The MISA licence that lets a foreign owner hold the company." |
| 2 | **Trade name reservation** | **48 hours** | "Your company name, reserved with the Ministry of Commerce." *(in parallel with step 1)* |
| 3 | **Articles and notarisation** | **72 hours** | "Articles of association drafted, signed and notarised." |
| 4 | **Commercial registration** | **24 hours** | "The CR is issued. Your company legally exists." |
| 5 | **Government account activation** | **24 hours** | "Tax, social insurance and labour portals switched on." |
| 6 | **After incorporation** | — | "GM visa and bank account, tracked separately." |

And the footnote, which matters as much as the table **[VERBATIM]**:

> Steps 1 and 2 run in parallel. The durations above are the government's, not ours.

## Why it adds to 15

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** The arithmetic is worth having explicitly, because a prospect will do it:

- Steps 1 and 2 run together, so the front half costs **10 working days**, not 12.
- Steps 3, 4 and 5 run in sequence: 72h + 24h + 24h ≈ **5 working days**.
- 10 + 5 = **15 working days.**

**The parallelism is the entire mechanism**, and the blog says so directly **[VERBATIM — blog]**:

> "Running the MISA licence review and the trade name reservation at the same time, instead of waiting for one to finish before starting the other, is what compresses the front half of the process."

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** Note what this means competitively: there is no secret and no privileged access. The claim is that they do not waste the gaps. A proposal should say that plainly rather than implying influence — implying influence is both off-brand and, with a government process, faintly alarming.

## What the 15 days does and does not include

**[VERBATIM — blog]**, and this is the most important paragraph in the process content:

> "Start Saudi's promise is specific: once your documents are complete, Taajeel takes your company from a standing start to a fully activated commercial registration in 15 working days, meaning Saudi business days rather than calendar days."

**Included:** MISA licence · trade name reservation · articles of association drafted and notarised · commercial registration · government account activation.

**Excluded — and the exclusions are load-bearing:**

1. **Everything before submission.** Document gathering, translation, notarisation, apostille or embassy attestation. **[VERBATIM — blog]** *"Document readiness before submission"* is named as the single biggest cause of delay for foreign applicants, and this sits on the client's side of the line.
2. **The GM visa.** Tracked separately, own timeline.
3. **The bank account.** Tracked separately, own timeline, and the blog is blunt that bank compliance *"can take as long as registration itself"*.

**[SOURCED]** "Working days" means **Saudi business days** — Sunday to Thursday. For a client in Europe or the Americas this is a real and frequently missed detail: the Saudi week does not overlap with theirs on Friday, and their Monday-to-Friday planning will drift. **[EXTRAPOLATED]** 15 Saudi working days is **three calendar weeks**, assuming no public holidays. A proposal that gives a calendar date rather than a working-day count is more useful to a foreign client and is exactly the kind of "showing the path" move this brand is built on. Watch for Ramadan and the two Eids, when government processing slows.

## It is a promise, not a guarantee

**[VERBATIM — blog]**, in answer to "Is 15 working days a government-guaranteed SLA or a service promise?":

> "It is a service promise, not a legal guarantee from any ministry."

And **[VERBATIM — blog]**:

> "The pace of each government step is outside any provider's control; no provider can make MISA approve a licence faster than MISA processes it."

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** Every proposal must carry this distinction explicitly. It is the brand's own framing, it pre-empts the procurement question, and omitting it would be the clearest possible breach of the voice. **[TO CONFIRM: what happens if it slips.]** No remedy, credit or escalation path is published. An enterprise RFP will ask. See OPEN-QUESTIONS Q14.

## What Start Saudi actually controls

**[SOURCED]** The blog is precise about this, and it is the honest version of the sales argument:

> Document readiness before each step opens · whether the next filing goes in immediately after the prior one clears · whether steps 1 and 2 genuinely run in parallel.

That is the whole list. **[EXTRAPOLATED]** For a proposal, this is more persuasive than a claim of speed, because it is checkable and because it tells the client what to ask a competitor.

## What slows an application down

**[SOURCED]** From the timeline post. Most of these sit outside the 15-day window, which is the point:

| Cause | Whose side | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Document readiness** | Client | Attestation, notarisation and certified translation happen *before* submission. Named as the biggest variable. |
| **Regulated or restricted activities** | Neither | Some sectors need approvals beyond the standard MISA licence. |
| **Incomplete or inconsistent filings** | Start Saudi / client | Trade name clashes or mismatched articles send a step back into the queue. |
| **Bank account compliance** | Bank | Runs entirely outside registration; *"can take as long as registration itself"*. |

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** In proposal terms this table is worth more than a testimonial. It is the thing a client cannot get from a competitor's brochure, and offering it unprompted is the clearest demonstration of *"we run the whole path"* that a document can make.

## Why competitors' timelines disagree

**[SOURCED]** The blog's framing, which is the ready-made answer to "Company X says 5 days":

Different providers measure different spans — a single government step (days); the core incorporation steps together (≈15 working days); the full path to an operating business including visa and bank account (longer); or that plus post-registration compliance (longer still).

**[VERBATIM — blog]**:

> "Before comparing timelines between providers, check which span is actually being quoted rather than assuming the numbers describe the same thing."

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** Reproduce this in any proposal that is competing on a shortlist. It reframes the comparison without disparaging anyone, which is exactly this brand's competitive register.

## Registered vs operating

**[VERBATIM — blog]**:

> "Treat 'company registered' and 'company operating' as two different milestones."

And **[VERBATIM — blog]**, from the post-CR post:

> "Your Saudi CR proves your company exists, not that it can trade yet."

**[SOURCED]** The suggested planning sequence:

1. Gather and attest documents **before engaging anyone** — this sits outside the 15-day clock.
2. Once the MISA licence and trade name are filed, start the conversations that do not need a CR.
3. Once the CR is issued (around day 15), proceed with visa, accounts and bank account.

**[VERBATIM — blog]**: *"If your plans depend on a specific date, such as a lease or a hire's start date, build in buffer for document preparation and for the post-CR steps."*

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** This is the paragraph that resolves most RFP timeline conflicts, because a stated launch date is almost always an *operating* date while a quoted registration timeline is a *registered* date. See `proposal/section-briefs.md` — it is the intended resolution of the deliberate timeline ambiguity in `sample-rfp.md`.

## The realistic end-to-end picture

**[EXTRAPOLATED — this whole table is inference, clearly marked as such, built by assembling sourced components. Do not present the totals as Start Saudi's published figures.]**

| Phase | Who leads | Realistic elapsed | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Document gathering, translation, attestation/apostille | **Client** | **[TO CONFIRM: 1–6 weeks]** — varies enormously by country and by whether it is a Hague apostille or a full embassy chain | Named as the biggest variable; no figure published |
| **The 15-working-day registration window** | Start Saudi / Taajeel | **≈3 calendar weeks** | 15 working days is published; the conversion to calendar weeks is the kit's, and assumes no public holidays |
| Bank account opening | Bank, with support | **2–3 weeks** after CR | *"Banks typically require 2-3 weeks"* |
| GM visa | Start Saudi / client | **[TO CONFIRM]** | No figure published |

**A foreign founder starting from nothing should plan on roughly two to three months from decision to operating**, not fifteen days — and telling them that in a proposal, rather than letting them discover it, is precisely what *"You don't know what you don't know"* commits this brand to. See OPEN-QUESTIONS Q15 and Q16 for the two missing durations.

## ⚠️ Taajeel's own timeline does not match the 15 working days

**[SOURCED — Taajeel's Havenstone proposal, June 2026. This is now the highest-priority open question in the kit: Q35.]**

Taajeel's client-facing Time Frame slide for a foreign-owned company formation — the same product Start Saudi sells — sets out **16 milestones with 15 durations between them**:

Workshop 1 day · Apply for MISA licence 5 · Submit AoA to the Saudi Business Center 5 · Get approval 2 · Notarise AoA 1 · Pay government fees 1 · Advertise AoA 1 · Receive Commercial Register 3 · Ratify partners' signatures 3 · ZATCA 1 · National Address 1 · HRSD 1 · Qiwa 1 · GOSI 2 · MUDAD 3 (Muqeem is the sixteenth milestone and carries no duration of its own).

**Total: 31 days, drawn sequentially, with no parallelism shown.** A further **47 days** for the GM residency path.

Start Saudi publishes **15 working days** — about 21 calendar days — for a path described in **6 steps**, with the MISA licence and trade name explicitly in parallel.

**These do not reconcile on their face.** The possibilities, none confirmed:

- **Start Saudi is a genuinely different product** — a standardised fast track, where Havenstone was a bespoke engagement for a Bahraini corporate shareholder on the longer embassy-attestation route.
- **Taajeel's chart is conservative and quotes calendar days**, and the infographic simply cannot express parallelism, so sequential steps are drawn where concurrent ones happen.
- **They are measuring different spans** — which is precisely the failure Start Saudi's own blog warns about: *"check which span is actually being quoted rather than assuming the numbers describe the same thing."* Taajeel's 31 days includes a client workshop, signature ratification, and Muqeem and MUDAD registrations that the Start Saudi six-step path never mentions.

All three may be partly true. **What must not happen is a Start Saudi proposal promising 15 working days for work the parent company's own client-facing timeline puts at 31 days.**

**Until Q35 is answered:** quote the 15-working-day promise with its published conditions attached — from complete documents, registration only, GM visa and bank account tracked separately — and give the client a **calendar-date view** rather than a per-step duration table that would contradict Taajeel's. See `proposal/section-briefs.md` § 9.

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** The Taajeel step list is still valuable as an **annex** for a client who wants the detail: it names Muqeem, MUDAD and the ratification of partners' signatures, none of which appear on the Start Saudi site, and all of which are real.

## Reuse notes

- **The six-step table is the single most reusable asset in the kit.** It carries the proof, the structure and the honesty in one object. It should appear in essentially every proposal.
- **Never present it without the footnote.** *"The durations above are the government's, not ours"* is not a disclaimer to be tidied away; it is the line that makes the rest credible.
- **Always carve out the exclusions in the same breath** as the 15-day claim.
- **Convert to calendar dates for the client's own timezone and week** wherever a launch date is in play.
