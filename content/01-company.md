# Start Saudi — who they are

> **Sourcing key used throughout `content/`**
> **[VERBATIM]** — reproduced word for word from a source. Safe to quote in a proposal.
> **[SOURCED]** — the fact is in a source; the wording here is the kit's.
> **[EXTRAPOLATED]** — an inference from sources. Do not present as fact without checking.
> **[TO CONFIRM: …]** — not available in any source. Every one is logged in `OPEN-QUESTIONS.md`.

## In one line

**[SOURCED]** Start Saudi registers Saudi companies for foreign founders and foreign companies entering the Saudi market, run end to end and signed remotely, with a published promise of **15 working days** from complete documents to a registered company with active government accounts.

*(Note the wording. Not "an operating company" — the brand's own instruction is to treat registered and operating as two different milestones, and a summary line is the easiest place to lose that distinction. See `04-process.md`.)*

## The proposition, in their own words

**[VERBATIM]** — homepage hero, in order:

> **Your Saudi company, registered in 15 working days**
>
> We run the whole path. Investment licence, trade name, articles of association, notarisation, commercial registration, government accounts. You sign from wherever you are.

Three claims are doing all the work there, and a proposal should carry all three:

1. **A number, not an adjective.** Not "fast" — fifteen working days.
2. **Scope, stated as a list.** "The whole path" is immediately itemised, so it can't be read as marketing.
3. **Remote.** "You sign from wherever you are" removes the objection the reader was already forming.

## The problem they say they solve

**[VERBATIM]** — homepage, section heading and body:

> **You don't know what you don't know**
>
> "Am I missing a deadline? Is there a payment I was supposed to make? Is something up for renewal that nobody told me about?"
>
> Most founders entering Saudi Arabia can see the opportunity clearly and cannot see the path at all. That gap is the whole problem, and it is expensive.

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** This is the strategic centre of the brand and it is unusual. Start Saudi does not position against *difficulty* — Saudi incorporation is not framed as hard. It positions against **uncertainty**: the client's fear is not that the work is beyond them, it is that they cannot see the path well enough to know what they have already missed. Read the FAQ, the process section and the qualification quiz and they are all answering the same anxiety.

A proposal in this voice therefore competes on **visibility**, not on effort or on price. The most on-brand thing a proposal can do is show the client the entire path — including the parts that are not Start Saudi's to control, and the parts that might go wrong.

## The Taajeel relationship

**[VERBATIM]** — appears in the site header and footer: **"Powered by Taajeel"**, linking to `taajeel.sa`.

**[SOURCED]** The structure, as far as the sources establish it:

- **Taajeel** is the parent and the operating company. Taajeel does the filings. The homepage says so directly: *"Taajeel runs the filings and you sign from where you are"* **[VERBATIM]**, and pricing is *"Taajeel's service fee and the government fees"* **[VERBATIM]**.
- **Start Saudi** is the outward-facing brand aimed specifically at **foreign founders and foreign companies**, handled remotely. It is newer and lighter in positioning than the parent.
- **All published credentials belong to Taajeel.** The track-record figures and the client logos are captioned *"Companies that launched with Taajeel"* **[VERBATIM]**. Start Saudi has no track record of its own, and does not claim one.

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** This is the single most important structural fact for proposal writing: **Start Saudi is the pitch and Taajeel is the proof.** A proposal that markets Start Saudi without the Taajeel endorsement is a new company with no history. A proposal that leads with Taajeel loses the foreign-founder positioning that is the whole reason Start Saudi exists. Every proposal must carry both, in that order.

**[TO CONFIRM: the legal relationship.]** Whether Start Saudi is a brand of Taajeel, a subsidiary, a joint venture, or a separate entity under agreement is *still* not established. It matters because a proposal names a contracting party. The privacy notice says only that Start Saudi *"operates this website"* and that Taajeel *receives submitted details*. What is now known is that **Taajeel contracts as `Taajeel Business Solutions Co. LLC`** and that its proposals are engagement letters signed by its General Manager — so the question is narrowly whether a Start Saudi proposal contracts under that same entity. See OPEN-QUESTIONS Q8.

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** That last point reframes the relationship. That is a coherent strategy, and it explains why Start Saudi's copy is so much sharper than the parent's — a single product can be described precisely in a way a service portfolio cannot.

### ⚠️ The voice clash

**[SOURCED]** Taajeel's proposals open their About section with:

> "Taajeel stands as a beacon of excellence in the business solutions landscape, seamlessly merging innovation with expertise to empower organizations to reach their full potential."

and close it with:

> "By choosing Taajeel, you are partnering with a leader dedicated to delivering results-driven solutions that align with your goals, backed by a proven track record of success and a commitment to innovation and excellence."

**Every content word in those two sentences is on the banned list in `voice.md`** — which was written from the Start Saudi site before these documents existed.

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** The two brands write in opposite registers, and a proposal-generation system sitting between them will drift toward the parent unless deliberately held. This is the central risk in the whole build. See `proposal/structure.md` § "What the proposals revealed that is a problem", and `voice.md` § 3a.

## Positioning

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** Read off the site's structure and word choices rather than from any positioning statement, which does not exist publicly:

| Dimension | Where Start Saudi sits | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Who it is for | Foreign founders and foreign companies, entering for the first time | The qualification quiz's first question is *"Where are you right now?"* with "Outside Saudi Arabia" first **[VERBATIM]** |
| Against | Uncertainty, not difficulty or cost | *"You don't know what you don't know"* **[VERBATIM]** |
| Proof | Volume and history, borrowed from Taajeel | 4,638+ companies; 26 client logos |
| Register | Plain, second-person, unhedged | See `voice.md` |
| Scope | The registration path end to end, with post-incorporation explicitly carved out | *"GM visa and bank account, tracked separately"* **[VERBATIM]** |
| On price | Transparent structure, no published numbers | *"You get both in writing before you commit"* **[VERBATIM]** |
| On risk | Names it first, including its own limits | *"The durations above are the government's, not ours"* **[VERBATIM]** |

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** The carve-outs are the positioning. Most competitors in this market advertise the widest possible scope; Start Saudi advertises a narrow, fast, guaranteed core and then says plainly what sits outside it. That is what makes the 15-day number credible, and it is why a proposal must never quietly widen the promise to win a deal.

## What they publish

**[SOURCED]** Eight long-form guides at `start-saudi.com/blog`, described as **[VERBATIM]** *"Plain-English guides for foreign founders on ownership, licences, timelines, costs and visas when opening a company in Saudi Arabia."*

They are unusually candid for marketing content — one is built entirely around the observation that published cost guides contradict each other, and another around the fact that competitor timelines measure different spans. **[EXTRAPOLATED]** The blog is a strategic asset for proposal writing, not just SEO: it is the best available record of how this company reasons in public, and several of its lines are the sharpest expression of the voice anywhere on the site.

## Language

**[SOURCED]** The site is **English only**. The header and footer both read *"Change language: English"* and offer no alternative; no Arabic appears on the homepage, the blog index, any of the eight posts, or the privacy notice.

Per the brief for this session, English is the primary proposal language and Arabic is a later implementation. See `brand/typography.md` for what a later Arabic build will need to settle.
