## Slide 7 · The question that decides everything

You asked whether a company of your type can be 100% foreign-owned. For most activities, yes. Saudi Arabia allows full foreign ownership under an investment licence from the Ministry of Investment. Technology, manufacturing, logistics and business consulting are all open by default.

**Your activity has not been classified yet, and the classification changes your capital requirement by two orders of magnitude.**

You described yourselves as 45% equipment, 55% service. Those two halves sit in different places:

- **Classified as services** — calibration, maintenance, technical support — there is **no minimum capital fixed in law**. Capital must be sufficient to achieve the company's objective, and there is no minimum commitment to invest during the first five years. In practice MISA expects capital proportionate to the activity, and the working benchmark commonly cited for a services company is around **SAR 500,000**.
- **Classified as retail or wholesale trading** at 100% foreign ownership, published guidance cites a capital requirement of **SAR 30,000,000** — roughly sixty times that benchmark.

The distinction between what the law fixes and what MISA expects in practice matters here, and we would rather you heard it from us than discovered it at filing.

We are not going to guess which applies. The answer depends on how the activity is registered, how the equipment reaches the client, and who holds title to it. Your brief does not give us enough to settle that. **We confirm the classification with MISA before you commit to anything.** It is the first thing on the discovery call, and the call is free.

What we can say now: one structure usually keeps you in the open band. The Saudi entity performs the service and calibration work, and equipment continues to be supplied from the UK under the existing arrangement. Whether that fits your client's requirement is a question for you.

**The second thing you did not ask about is on slide 10.** Someone has to be your General Manager in Saudi Arabia, and neither of you is going to be resident.

Your package begins **1 March 2027** and your client's supplier listing closes at the end of January. Working backwards from those two dates rather than forwards from today:

| When | What | Whose |
|---|---|---|

| **Mon 7 Dec → Sun 27 December** | The 15 working days. MISA licence and trade name, then articles, notarisation, CR, government accounts | Ours |
| **Sun 27 December** | **CR issued. The company legally exists.** Enough for your client's supplier listing, if their process needs only the CR | — |
| **Late Dec → mid/late January** | Bank account. 2–3 weeks after an active CR, run by the bank | The bank's |
| **1 March 2027** | You can invoice in riyals | — |

That leaves about five weeks of buffer before 1 March, and about five weeks between the CR and the end-of-January listing.

**Three things about this plan you should know.**

**Registered is not operating.** The CR proves your company exists. It does not mean you can trade. You cannot open a bank account, sign contracts, invoice clients or hire staff until the CR is issued — and invoicing in riyals needs the bank account, not the CR. Your end-of-January listing probably needs only the CR, but that is an assumption about your client's process. Ask them which they need.

**Attestation is the critical path and it is yours.** The fifteen days starts when your documents are complete. Getting them apostilled sits before that, on your side, and across every foreign registration it is the single biggest variable in how soon you finish. You are on the shorter route because the UK is an apostille country. Start it now; do not wait for 17 October.

**If 7 December slips, everything after it slips by the same amount.** There is buffer at the end, not in the middle.

## Slide 10 · Tracked separately — bank account and GM residency

Two things sit outside the fifteen days. We track both and we tell you where they stand, but they run on their own timelines and neither is in our control.

### The bank account

**2–3 weeks after your CR is active.** The bank runs its own compliance entirely outside the registration process, and it can take as long as the registration itself.

The bank will want the CR, the MISA licence, the articles, the National Address, the ZATCA registration and signatory ID — and, because your shareholder is a company, **Meridian's incorporation documents and board resolutions again, attested again, for the bank's own file**. Get two sets apostilled in December rather than one. It costs a little more and takes a round of attestation off your critical path in January.

Depending on the bank, opening the account may need someone to attend in person. We tell you which banks require it before you choose one.

### The General Manager

Your Saudi company must have a registered General Manager, and the GM is named in the articles of association — **step 3, not year one**. So this is a December decision, not a 2027 one.

## Why this passes the bar

Checked against the five-question test in `voice.md` § 5.

**1 · A number wherever a number exists.** Every duration is a figure. The government fees are itemised by authority. Where no number exists — the service fee — there is a bracket, not a hedge.

**2 · Every abstraction itemised within one sentence.** "We run the whole path" is followed immediately by six named steps. "Government accounts" is followed by ZATCA, GOSI, Qiwa, National Address, Chamber of Commerce.

**3 · Every promise followed by its boundary.** Fifteen working days → from complete documents → Saudi business days → a service promise, not a legal guarantee. The CR → and here is what it does not let you do. The 7 December plan → and if it slips, everything slips.

**4 · Nothing that could appear in a competitor's brochure.** The slide that decides the deal is slide 7, and it says *we do not know yet, here is why it matters, and here is when we will know*. No competitor writes that.

**5 · "We" only where a commitment follows.** "We confirm the classification with MISA before you commit." "We tell you which banks require it." "We do not estimate it." There is no sentence describing Start Saudi as anything.

### What it deliberately does not do

- **No price is invented.** Four `[TO CONFIRM]` brackets survive into the shipped document. To a reader who has already lost a deposit to an adviser who went quiet, an invented figure is unrecoverable — and a visible bracket is the honest version of the brand's own promise that you get both parts in writing before you commit.
- **No competing day-by-day timeline.** Slide 8 carries Start Saudi's own six published step durations, which is where they belong. Slide 9 then gives **calendar dates and milestones** rather than a second per-step sequence — because any such sequence would either reproduce Taajeel's 31-day chart, contradicting the fifteen-day promise, or invent one to fit. See `structure.md` and Q35.
- **No adjectives about Taajeel.** Slide 4 contains the relationship, four numbers and five client names. It contains no claim about quality. That is the single largest departure from the three real Taajeel proposals, and it is deliberate.
- **The activity classification is not guessed.** It would have been easy, and more comfortable, to write "your activity falls in the open band". The brief does not support it. Slide 7 says so and puts the resolution on the free discovery call — which is exactly what the homepage already promises.
