## The four headline figures

**[VERBATIM]** — homepage, section "Taajeel's track record":

| Figure | As published |
|---|---|
| **4,638+** | companies opened |
| **SAR 5.1B+** | attracted in capital and investment |
| **407,000+** | services delivered for clients |
| **15 working days** | to launch |

### How to use them

- **Always attribute to Taajeel.** The section heading is literally "Taajeel's track record". A proposal that presents these as Start Saudi's own is making a false claim about a company whose own track record is not published anywhere. **[TO CONFIRM: Start Saudi's founding date — Q12.]** Nothing in any source establishes how old the brand is; `01-company.md` notes only that the public site reads as a soft launch.
- **Keep the `+`.** It is doing real work: these are floors, not point estimates, and dropping it converts a careful claim into a precise one that will age badly.
- **Keep the exact figures.** "4,638+" is more persuasive than "over 4,600" precisely because it is odd and specific. Do not round it in the name of readability.
- **"407,000+ services delivered" needs a gloss.** Standing next to "4,638 companies", it invites the reader to divide and get 88 services per company, which is implausible and makes the reader distrust both numbers. **[TO CONFIRM: what counts as a "service".]** Almost certainly a per-transaction count across a much broader business than company registration — renewals, visa filings, government portal actions — but this is inference. See OPEN-QUESTIONS Q11. Until it is answered, either omit this figure from proposals or present it with a one-line explanation of what it counts.
- **[EXTRAPOLATED] The "SAR 5.1B+ in capital and investment" figure is the one a sophisticated reader will probe.** It is presumably the aggregate declared capital of companies Taajeel registered — i.e. capital its clients brought, not capital Taajeel raised or deployed. Phrase it so that distinction is unmistakable. "SAR 5.1B+ of capital has entered Saudi Arabia through companies Taajeel registered" is defensible; "Taajeel has attracted SAR 5.1B+" is not, and is close to what the site itself says.

### Verification status

**[SOURCED]** Searches for these figures returned no independent coverage — no press, no directory listing, no regulator record corroborating them. That is not evidence they are wrong; small B2B service firms are rarely covered. It does mean **no proposal should describe them as "verified", "audited" or "independently confirmed".** They are what Taajeel publishes about itself, and should be framed exactly that way. See OPEN-QUESTIONS Q12 — a single MISA or Ministry of Commerce agent licence number would let a serious prospect verify the firm, and is worth more in an enterprise proposal than any of the four numbers above.

## The named case study

**[SOURCED]** **Dr. Sulaiman Alsalameh**, founder of **ChiefNest**, United States. Presented on the homepage under the heading **[VERBATIM]** *"A founder who was where you are"*, with a video.

Two lines are quoted from him **[VERBATIM]**:

> "You don't know what you don't know. And that feeling sucks."
>
> "I went to a few places first and lost money."

And the site adds, in its own voice **[VERBATIM]**:

> Sulaiman is a Taajeel client and a partner in Start Saudi.

### How to use it

- **The disclosure is not optional.** "A partner in Start Saudi" is stated openly on the homepage, and a proposal that presents him as an arm's-length customer reference while the public site discloses the interest would be doing something the brand itself refuses to do. **Reproduce the disclosure in the same sentence as the testimonial.** It costs less credibility than being caught omitting it, and the candour is on-brand.
- **The second quote is the more useful one.** "I went to a few places first and lost money" is a competitive claim made by a customer rather than by the vendor, and it is the only piece of evidence in the entire corpus that speaks to what happens when a founder chooses badly. In a proposal it belongs next to a discussion of scope and price, not in a testimonial box at the end.
- **[TO CONFIRM: the story behind it.]** What ChiefNest does, what was actually registered, when, how long it took, and what "a few places" refers to are all unknown. A case study with an outcome — *"ChiefNest, a US company, registered in X working days after Y months of false starts"* — would be worth more than the four headline numbers combined, and it is available for the asking since the subject is a partner in the business. See OPEN-QUESTIONS Q13. **This is the highest-value single item in the whole open-questions list.**

## Summary: what a proposal can safely claim today

**Can claim, with attribution to Taajeel:** 4,638+ companies registered; SAR 5.1B+ of client capital into the Kingdom; 26 named clients spanning conglomerates to owner-operators, including PKF Global and Grand View Research; a named founder case study, with the partnership disclosed; a 15-working-day service promise, described as a promise.

**Can also now claim, from the proposals:** the contracting entity and its verifiable commercial registration — Taajeel Business Solutions Co. LLC, CR 1010941670, Riyadh; a named General Manager who signs; and a documented, priced service range extending well beyond formation.

**Cannot claim on current evidence:** anything as Start Saudi's own record; any figure described as verified or audited; any client logo, until permission is confirmed; **any guarantee, SLA or remedy — the standard Terms provide none**; any insurance position; any statement about team size, tenure or agent licensing.
