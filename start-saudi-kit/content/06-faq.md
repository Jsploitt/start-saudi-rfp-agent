# FAQ — questions founders ask first

The six homepage FAQs are reproduced **verbatim and in the order the site presents them**, because they are among the cleanest examples of the voice in the corpus (see `voice.md`). Below them, further questions drawn from the eight blog posts, then the questions a proposal will be asked that have no published answer.

---

## The six, as published

**[VERBATIM]** — homepage section *"Questions founders ask first"*:

### Can a foreigner own 100% of a Saudi company?

> For most activities, yes. Saudi Arabia allows full foreign ownership under an investment licence from the Ministry of Investment. A small number of activities are restricted or need extra approvals, and we confirm yours before you commit.

### Do I need a Saudi partner?

> Not for most activities. With a MISA investment licence a foreign founder can own the company outright. A few restricted activities still need a Saudi partner or a specific licence, which we check on the call.

### Do I have to travel to Saudi Arabia to set it up?

> The registration steps do not require you to be in the country. Taajeel runs the filings and you sign from where you are. Opening the bank account after incorporation may need a visit depending on the bank, and we tell you upfront.

### What does it cost?

> Two parts: Taajeel's service fee and the government fees for the licence, registration and other filings. Government fees are set by the authorities and vary by activity and company type. You get both in writing before you commit, and the discovery call is free.

### How long does it really take?

> 15 working days from complete documents to a registered company with active government accounts. The investment licence and trade name run in parallel first, then articles, notarisation, commercial registration and government accounts. The GM visa and bank account come after and are tracked separately.

### What can I not do on a standard iqama?

> A standard iqama on its own does not allow you to own a company. Your route runs through MISA registration, which is a real path with its own requirements. Leave your email in the form and we send you the steps.

### What the six have in common

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** Worth noticing, because it is the pattern a generated FAQ section must reproduce:

- **Every one opens with a direct answer** — "For most activities, yes." "Not for most activities." "15 working days." No preamble, no restatement of the question.
- **Every one names its exception in the second or third sentence.** Not one is a clean yes.
- **Four of the six end on a commitment**: "we confirm yours before you commit", "which we check on the call", "we tell you upfront", "the discovery call is free". The exception is offered *and* the responsibility for resolving it is taken, in the same breath.
- **Average length: three sentences.** None exceeds four.

## Further questions, answered from the blog

**[SOURCED]**, with verbatim strings marked. See `SOURCES.md` for the eight post URLs.

**What exactly is the difference between a MISA licence and a CR?**
**[VERBATIM — blog]** *"A MISA licence is the Ministry of Investment's permission for a foreign investor to own a business in Saudi Arabia. A Commercial Registration, or CR, is the Ministry of Commerce's registration of that business as an actual legal entity."* You need the first before the second, and a foreign-owned company generally needs both. Saudi and GCC nationals forming a wholly Saudi- or GCC-owned company skip the MISA step.

**What can I do with a MISA licence but no CR?**
**[VERBATIM — blog]** *"A MISA licence permits foreign ownership in principle, but you cannot open a bank account, sign contracts, invoice clients, or hire staff until your Commercial Registration is issued."*

**Which activities actually need a Saudi partner?**
**[SOURCED]** Regulated professional services — legal practice, accounting and audit, tax advisory, some engineering and architectural consultancy. **[VERBATIM — blog]** *"Public guidance commonly cites a minimum Saudi shareholding of around 25%, though the precise figure depends on the specific activity."*

**Which activities are closed entirely?**
**[SOURCED]** Upstream oil and gas exploration and production; military equipment manufacturing; certain Hajj and Umrah services within the Makkah and Madinah boundaries; real estate within the boundaries of the Two Holy Mosques. **[VERBATIM — blog]** these are *"activities where foreign investment is restricted or not permitted at all, regardless of ownership structure."*

**Which activities are straightforwardly open?**
**[SOURCED]** Technology, e-commerce, general trading, manufacturing, logistics, tourism, business consulting. **[VERBATIM — blog]** *"100% foreign ownership is generally the default position for a company operating in the Kingdom."*

**How do I sign if I am not in Saudi Arabia?**
**[SOURCED]** Through a Power of Attorney — **[VERBATIM — blog]** *"a signed document in which you, as the founder or the signatory for a parent company, name a specific person to act for you on specific matters."* It must be **specific, not general**, and must name the exact acts authorised. See `02-services/00-document-preparation.md`.

**Do I need an apostille or an embassy chain?**
**[SOURCED]** Depends on your country. Hague Apostille members: **[VERBATIM — blog]** documents *"can be apostilled in your home country and then submitted for attestation by Saudi authorities, without a separate Saudi embassy visit."* Non-members: notarisation at home → home Ministry of Foreign Affairs → Saudi embassy → Saudi Ministry of Foreign Affairs. Either way this **[VERBATIM — blog]** *"sits outside Taajeel's 15-working-day registration promise"* and is *"the thing to start early"*.

**Can I be the General Manager myself?**
**[VERBATIM — blog]** *"The GM does not have to be a hired manager. If you own the company, you can list yourself as GM."*

**Do I personally need residency to own a Saudi company?**
**[SOURCED]** No. Residency and ownership are legally separate. You can appoint a GM who is already resident, or become the resident GM yourself.

**Is an "investor visa" a real thing?**
**[SOURCED]** The blog's position is that the phrase collapses three different products: the **GM visa** (a work permit for the company's General Manager), the **routine investor iqama** (residency for partners/owners on a MISA-licensed registration), and **formal investor residency** (a high-bar track within Premium Residency, requiring minimum capital investment, minimum job creation within a defined window, and an application fee). Premium Residency is **[VERBATIM — blog]** *"personal: it is not attached to an employer or to a specific commercial registration."*

**Branch or a new LLC?**
**[SOURCED]** A branch is an extension of the parent with no separate legal personality — the parent stays directly liable — and **[VERBATIM — blog]** *"can only be licensed to do what your home-country company already does there."* An LLC is a distinct Saudi entity with liability generally limited to its own capital. An LLC suits a company with no existing operations anywhere, because **[VERBATIM — blog]** *"there is no parent history to authenticate and defend to a regulator."*

**What happens the day after my CR is issued?**
**[SOURCED]** Several files open automatically: **[VERBATIM — blog]** *"Your establishment file on Qiwa opens automatically when your CR is issued."* GOSI, ZATCA, the National Address and Chamber of Commerce registration likewise. Automatic opening is not the same as being configured or compliant. See `02-services/05-government-account-activation.md`.

**How long does a bank account take?**
**[SOURCED]** **[VERBATIM — blog]** *"Yes, a foreign-owned company can open a business bank account in Saudi Arabia once its CR is active."* Banks typically require **2–3 weeks** and run compliance entirely outside the registration process; it *"can take as long as registration itself"*.

**Is there a minimum capital?**
**[SOURCED]** For a general LLC, no fixed minimum post-2023 reform — capital must be **[VERBATIM — blog]** *"sufficient to achieve the company's objective"*. Specific structures and activities carry thresholds; see `05-pricing.md`, and note the **SAR 30,000,000** figure cited for 100% foreign-owned retail and wholesale trading.

**Can I speed it up?**
**[SOURCED]** Yes, but not by pushing the government. **[VERBATIM — blog]** *"Since the 15-working-day timeline assumes your documents are already complete, preparing your paperwork before you begin is the most direct way to bring your overall timeline forward."*

## Questions with no published answer

**[TO CONFIRM — all of these.]** A proposal, unlike a website, will be asked them. Each is logged in `OPEN-QUESTIONS.md`.

- What happens if the 15 working days is missed? Is there a credit, a remedy, an escalation? *(Q14)*
- How much does it cost? *(Q17)*
- Are government fees passed through at cost? *(Q17)*
- What are the payment terms and milestones? *(Q18)*
- Is VAT charged to a non-resident client? *(Q19)*
- Is Taajeel a licensed agent, and what is the licence number? *(Q12)*
- Who is the named point of contact, and in what timezone? *(Q20)*
- What is the process if MISA rejects or queries the application? *(Q21)*
- Which banks does Taajeel work with, and what are their relative timelines? *(Q22)*
- What happens after year one — renewals, annual compliance, and is there an ongoing service? *(Q23)*
- How is client data handled under the Saudi PDPL? *(Q24)*
- Is there professional indemnity insurance? *(Q25)*
- Can Taajeel support the activity-code selection, and who is accountable if the wrong code is chosen? *(Q26)*

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** The last one is quietly the most important commercially. Activity codes determine ownership rules, capital thresholds and which approvals apply — the SAR 30m trading threshold turns on it. A proposal that shows the client the code being proposed, and says plainly what follows from it, is doing the most valuable thing this company claims to do.

## Reuse note

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** Do not paste all six homepage FAQs into every proposal. Pick the two or three the RFP actually raises and answer them in the RFP's own terms — a proposal that answers questions the client did not ask reads like a brochure, which is the opposite of this brand. The FAQ content is best used as **raw answers to be re-pointed**, with the structure preserved: direct answer, then the exception, then who resolves it.
