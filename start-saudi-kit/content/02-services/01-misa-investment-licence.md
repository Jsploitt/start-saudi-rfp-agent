# MISA investment licence

**Step 1 of 5. 10 working days. Runs in parallel with the trade name reservation. The longest step, and the gate everything else waits on.**

---

## What it is

**[VERBATIM]** — homepage: *"The MISA licence that lets a foreign owner hold the company."*

**[VERBATIM — blog]** — the fuller definition:

> "A MISA licence is the Ministry of Investment's permission for a foreign investor to own a business in Saudi Arabia."

**[SOURCED]** It is required for any company with foreign ownership and must come first. Saudi and GCC nationals forming a wholly Saudi- or GCC-owned company skip it entirely.

**The critical limitation** **[VERBATIM — blog]**:

> "A MISA licence permits foreign ownership in principle, but you cannot open a bank account, sign contracts, invoice clients, or hire staff until your Commercial Registration is issued."

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** Clients routinely believe the MISA licence is the finish line, because it is the hardest-sounding step and it takes the longest. It is not. A proposal should make the licence/CR distinction explicit — it is the most common conceptual error in this market, and correcting it early is cheap.

## How long it takes

**[VERBATIM]** — homepage: **10 working days**, running in parallel with step 2.

**[SOURCED]** But the blog is careful not to over-claim:

> No single figure applies to all applications. Speed depends on submission completeness, sector, and current Ministry processing volumes.

And **[VERBATIM — blog]**:

> "The pace of each government step is outside any provider's control; no provider can make MISA approve a licence faster than MISA processes it."

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** Present the 10 working days as the published planning figure and reproduce the caveat in the same paragraph. That pairing is the house style.

## What it depends on

- **Complete, correctly attested documents.** The clock does not start otherwise.
- **The activity being open to foreign ownership** — see the bands below.
- **Ministry processing volumes**, which no one controls.
- **Sector approvals** where the activity is regulated; some sectors need approvals beyond the standard licence.

## Ownership bands — the decisive question

**[SOURCED]**

**Open — 100% foreign ownership is the default.** **[VERBATIM — blog]** *"100% foreign ownership is generally the default position for a company operating in the Kingdom."*
Technology · e-commerce · general trading · manufacturing · logistics · tourism · business consulting.
**[SOURCED]** A single foreign founder may own 100% alone through a single-shareholder LLC.

**Restricted — Saudi shareholding required.**
Legal practice · accounting and audit · tax advisory · some engineering consultancy · some architectural consultancy.
**[VERBATIM — blog]** *"Public guidance commonly cites a minimum Saudi shareholding of around 25%, though the precise figure depends on the specific activity."*

**Capital-gated.** **[SOURCED]** Retail and wholesale trading at 100% foreign ownership: **SAR 30,000,000** cited. See `05-pricing.md`.

**Closed.** **[SOURCED]** Upstream oil and gas exploration and production · military equipment manufacturing · certain Hajj and Umrah services within the Makkah and Madinah boundaries · real estate within the boundaries of the Two Holy Mosques. **[VERBATIM — blog]** these are *"activities where foreign investment is restricted or not permitted at all, regardless of ownership structure."*

## Fees

**[SOURCED, contradictory]** The Start Saudi blog reports that sources conflict: some 2026 guides state the MISA fee is waived entirely, others cite SAR 2,000 upfront plus an annual renewal reaching SAR 60,000–62,000 depending on licence type. The post notes **[VERBATIM — blog]** *"Saudi Arabia has used fee waivers as a temporary incentive before"*, leaving current status uncertain.

**Do not quote a MISA fee in a proposal.** State that government fees are set by the authorities, that current published guidance on this specific fee conflicts, and that the confirmed figure comes with the written quotation. **[EXTRAPOLATED]** Saying so is more persuasive than a number — it is a live demonstration of the brand's central claim.

## What commonly goes wrong

- **Wrong or too-narrow activity classification.** **[EXTRAPOLATED]** The highest-consequence error available: it determines ownership rules, capital thresholds and which approvals apply. A company that classifies as trading rather than services can find itself facing the SAR 30m threshold. See OPEN-QUESTIONS Q26 for who is accountable for the choice.
- **Documents not fully attested**, or the apostille route assumed incorrectly.
- **Restricted activity discovered late** — the client has planned a wholly-owned entity and needs a Saudi partner.
- **Sector approval not anticipated** where the activity is regulated.
- **Mismatch between the parent's actual business and the activity applied for**, especially for a branch.

## Proposal notes

**[EXTRAPOLATED]**
- **State the activity band explicitly and early.** "Your activity falls in the open band; 100% foreign ownership applies" is the most reassuring sentence available — and if it is not true, the client needs to know on page one.
- **Name the proposed activity code or classification** and what follows from it. It makes the analysis checkable.
- Pair the 10-working-day figure with the ministry-volumes caveat, every time.
- Do not describe this step as approval of the business. It is permission for a foreign owner to hold it.
