# Articles of association and notarisation

**Step 3 of 5. 72 hours. The first step on the critical path after the MISA licence clears — and the point at which the client's structure becomes permanent.**

---

## What it is

**[VERBATIM]** — homepage: *"Articles of association drafted, signed and notarised."*

**[SOURCED]** The company's constitutional document: shareholders and their holdings, capital, objects, management and the authority of the General Manager. Drafted, signed by or on behalf of the shareholders, and notarised. **[SOURCED]** The blog describes it as *"a short step after steps 1 and 2"*.

## What Start Saudi does

**[SOURCED]**
- Drafts the articles consistent with the MISA licence and the reserved trade name
- Arranges signature — **[VERBATIM]** *"You sign from wherever you are"*, executed in-Kingdom by the representative under the Power of Attorney
- Arranges notarisation

## What the client provides

**[SOURCED]**
- Final shareholding structure and the split between shareholders
- Share capital amount — see the thresholds in `05-pricing.md`
- **The named General Manager.** **[VERBATIM — blog]**: *"The GM does not have to be a hired manager. If you own the company, you can list yourself as GM."*
- **[EXTRAPOLATED]** Decisions on the GM's authority and on any reserved matters
- The executed Power of Attorney authorising signature of the articles specifically — **[SOURCED]** a general POA will not do

## How long it takes

**[VERBATIM]** — homepage: **72 hours.**

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** Unlike the trade name, this is on the critical path. Its three days are three of the five that follow the licence.

## What it depends on

- The MISA licence being issued and the trade name reserved
- A POA that specifically authorises signing the articles
- **[EXTRAPOLATED]** The shareholding and capital being genuinely settled — this is the last comfortable moment to change them

## What commonly goes wrong

**[SOURCED]** The blog names **"mismatched AoA"** among the failures that send steps *"back into queues"*.

**[EXTRAPOLATED]**
- **Inconsistency with the MISA licence or the reserved name** — names, shareholding or activity differing between documents
- **The GM decision left open**, stalling the draft. A surprising number of clients have not made it, because nobody asked them to
- **Capital set without regard to the activity's practical expectations** — see `05-pricing.md` on statutory minimums versus what MISA expects in practice
- **The POA does not cover signing the articles**, discovered at notarisation
- **Late shareholder changes.** Changing the structure after this point means amending a notarised constitutional document rather than editing a draft

## Proposal notes

**[EXTRAPOLATED]**
- **Surface the GM decision in the proposal, not at step 3.** It has two consequences the client will not have connected: it determines who needs the GM visa, and therefore whether someone has to become resident. For a solo founder the answer is usually "you", which means residency, which means a timeline they had not planned for. Flagging it early is high-value and costs a sentence.
- **Confirm shareholding and capital in writing before this step opens**, and say in the proposal that you will. The brand's pattern is naming the decision point before arriving at it.
- Keep the description short. Clients do not want a lesson in constitutional drafting; they want to know what they must decide and by when.
