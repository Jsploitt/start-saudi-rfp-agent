# Branch of a foreign company

**An alternative to forming a new LLC, not an additional step. The structural decision that must be made before anything else is filed.**

---

## What it is

**[SOURCED]** A branch is an **extension of an existing foreign company** with **no separate legal personality**. The parent remains directly liable for its obligations.

Against that, a **new LLC** is a distinct Saudi legal entity with liability **[VERBATIM — blog]** *"generally limited to the LLC's own capital"*, even at 100% foreign ownership.

**The defining constraint** **[VERBATIM — blog]**:

> "A branch can only be licensed to do what your home-country company already does there."

## When each fits

**[SOURCED]** A **branch** suits a company that:

- Already operates elsewhere with established trade credentials
- Wants Saudi counterparts to recognise the **same entity** they already deal with
- Plans activities **matching its current home-country licensing**
- Has no intention of adding co-owners

**[SOURCED]** A **new LLC** works better for a company with no existing operations anywhere, because **[VERBATIM — blog]** *"there is no parent history to authenticate and defend to a regulator."*

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** That last clause is the counter-intuitive and genuinely useful insight: a branch sounds like the lighter option — no new company, just an extension — and it is usually the heavier one. The parent's entire history has to be documented, authenticated and justified to a regulator. A newly formed LLC has no history to defend. **A proposal that explains this inversion is doing something a client will not have worked out for themselves.**

## What the client provides

**[SOURCED]** For a branch, the full parent-company pack, authenticated and translated into Arabic:

- Certificate of incorporation
- Memorandum and articles of association
- Certificate of good standing
- Board resolution approving the branch
- Power of attorney
- **Recent audited financial statements**

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** The audited financials are the item that catches people. A small foreign company that has never been audited cannot produce them on demand, and obtaining them is weeks of work with an accountant — which can by itself decide the branch-versus-LLC question on practical grounds.

## How long it takes

**[SOURCED]** The published benchmark is the same: **[VERBATIM — blog]** *"a Saudi entity live within 15 working days once your documents are complete."*

**[SOURCED]** But the pace *"typically depends on parent-company authentication speed rather than government processing"* — so for a branch the pre-clock phase is longer and more determinative than for an LLC.

## Capital

**[SOURCED, contradictory]** The blog states guidance is inconsistent: some sources cite a minimum share capital for a branch; others state branches need not inject local capital because **[VERBATIM — blog]** *"the parent's own financial standing substitutes for it"*. Thresholds vary by activity and should be confirmed with a licensed adviser.

**Do not state a branch capital figure in a proposal.** Describe the position as unsettled and say the confirmed requirement comes with the written quotation — consistent with how `05-pricing.md` handles the MISA fee.

## What commonly goes wrong

**[EXTRAPOLATED]**
- **Branch chosen for apparent simplicity**, then the authentication burden lands
- **Audited financial statements do not exist**, discovered after the structure was decided
- **The intended Saudi activity is not something the parent already does at home** — fatal to the branch route, and the constraint is absolute
- **Certificate of good standing has expired** by the time the chain completes
- **Liability implications not understood.** The parent is directly exposed; for many groups that alone rules it out and nobody raised it
- **Later desire to add a Saudi partner or investor**, which a branch cannot accommodate
