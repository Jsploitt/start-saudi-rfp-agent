## 2. The rules behind them

### Sentence construction

- **Short declaratives, and they land on the verb.** "The CR is issued. Your company legally exists." Two sentences, eight words, no adjective. The house length is roughly **8–18 words**; anything past 30 is off-brand.
- **Fragments are allowed when they are lists.** "GM visa and bank account, tracked separately." No verb, and it is the clearest line on the page.
- **One idea per sentence.** Where a competitor writes one sentence with three subordinate clauses, this brand writes three sentences. Compare: *"We run the whole path."* then the list, then *"You sign from wherever you are."*
- **The list is the proof.** Whenever a broad claim is made, the very next thing is an itemisation of it. "The whole path" is immediately followed by six named steps. Never let an abstraction stand alone for more than one sentence.

### Person and address

- **Second person throughout, and it is the client.** "You sign." "Your company." "You get both in writing." The reader is the subject of their own registration.
- **First person plural for the company, sparingly.** "We run the whole path." "we confirm yours before you commit." "we tell you upfront." Note that all three are commitments — **"we" is reserved for promises**, not for description. The brand never says "we are a leading provider."
- **Taajeel is named in the third person, as the operator.** "Taajeel runs the filings." "Taajeel's service fee." The brand does not blur the two.

### Tense and mood

- **Present indicative for what happens.** "The CR is issued." Not "will be issued", not "can be issued".
- **Imperative only in calls to action.** "See if we're a fit." "Sign from wherever you are."
- **No conditional hedging on things within their control.** There is no "we would typically aim to" anywhere on this site.

### How they handle uncertainty — the signature move

This is what makes the voice hard to imitate and easy to get wrong. The brand is **not** confident about everything. It is precisely confident about its own scope and **flatly, unhedged honest about everything outside it**. The pattern is consistent:

> **State the promise. Immediately name its boundary. Do not apologise for the boundary.**

- "15 working days" → "from complete documents" → "The durations above are the government's, not ours."
- "We run the whole path" → "GM visa and bank account, tracked separately."
- "15 working days" → "It is a service promise, not a legal guarantee from any ministry."
- "For most activities, yes" → "A small number of activities are restricted or need extra approvals, and we confirm yours before you commit."
- "The registration steps do not require you to be in the country" → "Opening the bank account after incorporation may need a visit depending on the bank, and we tell you upfront."

Note what the boundary is *not* accompanied by: no "unfortunately", no "please note that", no "whilst we endeavour to". The limit is stated in the same flat register as the promise, which is what makes both believable.

**A proposal that softens a limitation is more off-brand than one that gets a fact wrong.**

### How they talk about money

- **Structure before number.** "Two parts: Taajeel's service fee and the government fees." The shape of the price is explained before any figure.
- **Ownership of each part is attributed.** Government fees "are set by the authorities and vary by activity and company type" — i.e. not ours, not negotiable, not our margin.
- **The commitment is to writing, not to cheapness.** "You get both in writing before you commit." The brand never claims to be affordable, competitive, or good value.
- **[VERBATIM — blog]** "Government fees and adviser fees are two different things." The blog post on cost is built entirely around this distinction and around the observation that published guides disagree with each other. **Candour about the confusion is the sales argument.**

### How they talk about risk

- **Risk is named early and specifically, never buried.** The delay causes are itemised — document readiness, regulated activities, inconsistent filings, bank compliance — and the biggest one is attributed to the *client's* side of the line: **[VERBATIM — blog]** "Document readiness before submission."
- **They tell the client what will slow them down before the client asks.** That is the single most trust-generating move in the copy and a proposal should reproduce it.
- **They will say a competitor's number is not wrong, just measuring something else.** "check which span is actually being quoted rather than assuming the numbers describe the same thing." The brand competes by clarifying, not by disparaging.

### Register

- **Plain, not folksy.** The vocabulary is ordinary but the sentences are tight. It reads like a competent person explaining something, not like a brand being relatable.
- **Emotionally direct exactly once per section, then straight back to facts.** "And that feeling sucks" is the most informal moment on the entire site, it belongs to a quoted client rather than the brand, and the very next thing on the page is a table of durations. The emotional register is *earned and rationed*.
- **Technical terms are used, then immediately defined in one clause.** "The MISA licence that lets a foreign owner hold the company." "A Commercial Registration, or CR." Never a glossary, never an undefined acronym.
- **British spelling.** "licence" (noun), "notarisation", "organised". Consistent across the site and the blog. **Match it.**

## 3. Banned words and constructions

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** Inferred from what this brand conspicuously never says. None of the following appears in the homepage or privacy notice, which were retrieved as full transcriptions, or in the blog material retrieved — but note that the eight blog posts came back partly as summary rather than in full (see `SOURCES.md`), so this is a well-supported inference about the register, not a verified word-by-word audit of the whole corpus.

**Never use — consultancy filler**

> leverage · synergy · bespoke · tailored · holistic · seamless · seamlessly · end-to-end *(as an adjective — they write "the whole path")* · turnkey · one-stop shop · value-add · best-in-class · world-class · cutting-edge · state-of-the-art · robust · innovative · solutions *(as a noun for services)* · offerings · deliverables *(client-facing)* · ecosystem · journey *(they say "path")* · partner *(as a verb)* · empower · unlock · unleash · elevate · transform · navigate *(as in "navigate the complexities")* · streamline · optimise · strategic *(as a decorative adjective)*

**Never use — self-praise the brand has not earned in writing**

> leading · premier · trusted · renowned · award-winning · unrivalled · unparalleled · expert *(as a self-description)* · dedicated team · passionate · committed to excellence · proven track record *(they just state the number instead)*

**Never use — hedges that break the honesty contract**

> we endeavour to · we strive to · typically aim to · every effort will be made · subject to change without notice · in most cases *(as a way of avoiding stating which cases)* · please note that · unfortunately · we regret to inform · it should be borne in mind

**Never use — vagueness where a number exists**

> quickly · rapidly · in a timely manner · shortly · competitive pricing · affordable · cost-effective · minimal delay · hassle-free · stress-free · effortless

**Never use — false intimacy or false drama**

> exciting opportunity · thrilled · delighted · we're excited to · gateway to the Kingdom *(the parent's line, not this brand's)* · land of opportunity · your trusted partner on this journey

**Constructions to avoid**

- **Nominalisation.** "the facilitation of the registration process" → "we register the company".
- **Passive voice hiding the actor.** "the licence will be obtained" → "we file for the licence" or "MISA issues the licence".
- **Exclamation marks.** There are none on the site.
- **Rhetorical questions in the brand's own voice.** The site's questions belong to the client ("Am I missing a deadline?") or to a section heading ("Are we a fit?"). The brand never asks a question it then answers to sound clever.
- **Stacked adjectives.** "a fast, efficient, reliable service" is three words where the brand would give a number.
- **American spelling.**

## 3a. ⚠️ The parent company writes in the banned register

**This section was added after three real Taajeel proposals were supplied. It is the most important practical warning in the file.**

Everything above was derived from the Start Saudi website. Taajeel's own proposals — the documents a generated Start Saudi proposal will most resemble structurally — are written in exactly the register this file bans. **[VERBATIM]**, from the About Taajeel slide, identical in all three:

> "Taajeel stands as a beacon of excellence in the business solutions landscape, seamlessly merging innovation with expertise to empower organizations to reach their full potential. Rooted in a commitment to unparalleled quality, we provide comprehensive management consulting, administrative optimization, and strategic insights that drive success. At Taajeel, our mission transcends delivering services—we aspire to transform challenges into opportunities, fostering growth, resilience, and sustainable excellence for our clients in an ever-evolving Saudi market."

> "By choosing Taajeel, you are partnering with a leader dedicated to delivering results-driven solutions that align with your goals, backed by a proven track record of success and a commitment to innovation and excellence."

Count against § 3: *beacon of excellence · seamlessly · merging innovation with expertise · empower · unparalleled · comprehensive · solutions* (as a noun) *· drive success · transcends · transform challenges into opportunities · sustainable excellence · a leader · results-driven · proven track record · commitment to innovation and excellence*. Fifteen banned items across two short passages, and the second passage is a pure self-praise construction that the Start Saudi site never once attempts.

**Three things follow.**

1. **The "Before" examples in § 4 are not strawmen.** They were written before these documents were seen, and they turned out to be close paraphrases of the parent company's real copy. The rewrites in § 4 are therefore the actual job, not an illustration of it.

2. **A model drafting a Start Saudi proposal will drift toward this register**, because it is the register of the only real proposals in the corpus and because it is the default voice of B2B services writing generally. The drift will be invisible — it produces fluent, professional, entirely unobjectionable prose. **Fluency is the failure mode here, not the goal.**

3. **The About-the-company slide is where it will happen first.** That slide has no facts to anchor it unless you insist on some. The fix is structural rather than stylistic: allow it **no adjectives about Taajeel at all** — only what Start Saudi does, what Taajeel is, and the four track-record figures. See `proposal/section-briefs.md` § 4.

**What Taajeel's proposals get right, and should be kept:** the structure, the letter furniture, the client slide's discipline about named facts, the fee slide's units, the standing notes, and the Terms and Conditions verbatim. The skeleton is sound. It is the prose that must not survive.

## 4. Before and after

### Pair 1 — the scope paragraph

**Before** *(generic consultancy)*

> Our end-to-end company formation solution leverages our deep local expertise and established government relationships to deliver a seamless market-entry journey. We handle all aspects of the incorporation process on your behalf, allowing you to focus on your core business while we navigate the complexities of the Saudi regulatory landscape. Our dedicated team is committed to ensuring a hassle-free experience from start to finish.

**After** *(Start Saudi)*

> We run the whole path. Investment licence, trade name, articles of association, notarisation, commercial registration, government accounts. You sign from wherever you are.
>
> Two things sit outside that: the GM visa and the bank account. We track both, and we tell you where they stand, but they run on their own timelines after your CR is issued.

*What changed:* 63 words to 57, but the second version contains six named steps and two named exclusions where the first contained none. Every abstraction was either itemised or cut. The boundary was added — the first version's silence about the visa and the bank account is the kind of omission this brand exists not to make.

### Pair 2 — the timeline claim

**Before** *(generic consultancy)*

> We pride ourselves on our rapid turnaround times. Whilst every engagement is unique and timelines may vary depending on a number of factors, we typically aim to complete the registration process in an efficient and timely manner, and we will endeavour to keep you updated throughout.

**After** *(Start Saudi)*

> 15 working days, from complete documents to a registered company with active government accounts.
>
> The investment licence and the trade name run in parallel first, then articles, notarisation, commercial registration and government accounts. The durations are the government's, not ours — we control what is filed, how complete it is, and how fast the next step goes in after the last one clears.
>
> It is a service promise, not a legal guarantee from any ministry. The clock starts when your documents are complete; getting them attested sits before that, on your side, and it is usually the longest part of the whole thing.

*What changed:* the hedges are gone and the honesty went up, not down. The first version protects itself with vagueness and commits to nothing. The second gives a hard number, then gives away more genuinely inconvenient information than the client asked for — the promise is not a guarantee, the clock excludes attestation, and attestation is the client's problem. It is a stronger sales document precisely because of that.

### Pair 3 — the pricing section

**Before** *(generic consultancy)*

> We offer highly competitive and transparent pricing tailored to your specific requirements. Our flexible fee structure is designed to deliver exceptional value, and we would be delighted to discuss a bespoke quotation with you at your earliest convenience. Please do not hesitate to contact us to arrange an initial consultation.

**After** *(Start Saudi)*

> The price has two parts.
>
> Taajeel's service fee, for running the filings. And the government fees for the licence, the registration and the other filings — those are set by the authorities and they vary by activity and company type. They are not ours. `[TO CONFIRM: whether government fees are passed through at cost — Q17]`
>
> *(Note the bracket. "We do not mark them up" would be the stronger sentence, and it is exactly the claim this system must not generate: no source states it, and `05-pricing.md` logs it as Tier 1 open question Q17. A model paragraph that breaks the rule teaches the rule away.)*

> You get both in writing before you commit. The discovery call is free.

*What changed:* "competitive", "transparent", "exceptional value" and "bespoke" all disappear, and the paragraph becomes more persuasive without them. "Transparent" is replaced by an actual act of transparency — the fee is split and the part they don't control is attributed. Note also that the "After" is shorter and contains one real commitment ("in writing before you commit") where the "Before" contained none.

## 5. A five-question test for any generated paragraph

1. **Is there a number where a number exists?** If the paragraph says "quickly" and a figure is available, it fails.
2. **Is every abstraction itemised within one sentence?** "The whole path" is fine; "comprehensive support" is not.
3. **Does it state a boundary?** Almost every substantive claim on the site is followed by its limit. A paragraph of pure promise is off-brand.
4. **Could this sentence appear in a competitor's brochure unchanged?** If yes, cut it.
5. **Does it say "we" anywhere it is not making a commitment?** If so, rewrite in the second person or in the present indicative.

## 6. What this voice must not be pushed into

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** One risk worth naming. The voice is candid and unhedged, and candour is easy to over-rotate into either flippancy or, worse, into **stating things with more certainty than the sources support**. The brand is plain-spoken about what it knows and explicitly humble about what it does not control — "the durations above are the government's, not ours" is a *disclaimer*, delivered confidently.

In a generated proposal, that becomes: **a `[TO CONFIRM: …]` marker is more on-brand than a confident invented figure.** A company whose entire pitch is "you don't know what you don't know" cannot be the company that quietly fills a gap with a plausible number.
