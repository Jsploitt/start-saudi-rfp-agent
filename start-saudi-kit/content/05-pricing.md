# Pricing

**No Start Saudi or Taajeel price is published anywhere.** Not a service fee, not a range, not a starting-from figure. This file describes the **structure** of the price, which is published and is itself a selling point, and separately records the **third-party government fee figures** that the Start Saudi blog cites — with an explicit warning about how they are framed there.

**Nothing in this file may be presented to a client as a Start Saudi quotation.**

---

## The model, as published

**[VERBATIM]** — homepage FAQ, "What does it cost?":

> Two parts: Taajeel's service fee and the government fees for the licence, registration and other filings. Government fees are set by the authorities and vary by activity and company type. You get both in writing before you commit, and the discovery call is free.

**[VERBATIM — blog]**, from the cost post:

> "Government fees and adviser fees are two different things."

### The four commitments inside that

**[SOURCED]** Small paragraph, four separate promises, and a proposal should carry all four:

1. **The price separates into two parts**, named and attributed.
2. **Government fees are not Taajeel's** — "set by the authorities", varying "by activity and company type". **[EXTRAPOLATED]** This is an implicit no-markup claim. It is not stated as one. **[TO CONFIRM: are government fees passed through at cost, or marked up / bundled?](OPEN-QUESTIONS Q17)** An enterprise buyer will ask this in exactly those words.
3. **Both parts come in writing before commitment.** A pre-commitment written quotation is the actual promise, and it is stronger than most competitors offer.
4. **The discovery call is free.**

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** The brand competes on *legibility* of price, not on level of price. Nowhere does it say affordable, competitive, or good value — consistent with the banned-words list in `voice.md`. A proposal that introduces "competitively priced" is off-brand even if it is true.

## What the Taajeel proposals show

**[SOURCED — three real Taajeel proposals dated June–August 2026, supplied September 2026. See `SOURCES.md`.]**

Taajeel's actual quoted prices are now known. **They are Taajeel's, for specific scopes, to specific clients, on specific dates. They are not a Start Saudi price list and must never be presented as one.** What they establish is the *shape* of Taajeel's commercial practice, which is directly reusable.

### The closest comparable: Havenstone, June 2026

A Bahraini management consultancy, two foreign shareholders, establishing a foreign-owned LLC in Saudi Arabia — the same product Start Saudi sells.

| Item | Fee |
|---|---|
| **Set up foreign company & issue resident visa for main General Manager** | **SAR 40,000** |
| VAT at 15% | SAR 6,000 |

**Payment milestones: 50% on signing · 35% on MISA approval · 15% on issue of the commercial registration.**

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** Two of the three milestones are government outcomes the client can verify independently. That is a strong answer to the "adviser took my deposit and went quiet" anxiety, and a proposal should present it as answering that rather than as an administrative detail.

### Government fees, as Taajeel presents them

From Havenstone's separate government-fee slide, described there as **estimated**:

| Authority | Fee |
|---|---|
| Ministry of Investment (MISA licence) | **`#,###` — "Yet to be determined once billed will share with client"** |
| Ministry of Commerce | SAR 1,775 first year, then SAR 2,165 renewal |
| Qiwa platform subscription | SAR 1,265 per year |
| Muqeem platform | SAR 1,840 (SAR 1,265 subscription + SAR 575 for 2,500 points) |
| Riyadh Chamber of Commerce | SAR 350 (balance for issuing 10 letters) |
| **Total, first year** | **SAR 5,230\*** |

Separately, **Residence Iqama government fees: SAR 14,750 per person per year** — SAR 2,000 visa, SAR 9,600 HRSD work permit, SAR 650 Ministry of Interior, SAR 1,000 medical test, SAR 1,500 medical insurance.

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** Two things to notice, and both matter more than the numbers.

**Taajeel does not state the MISA fee either.** It prints `#,###` and promises the figure when billed. That is independent confirmation of the position this file already took — and it means a Start Saudi proposal that declines to quote the MISA fee is following house practice, not being evasive.

**The total carries an asterisk and the word "estimated".** SAR 1,775 + 1,265 + 1,840 + 350 = 5,230 exactly, so the total is the sum of the *known* items and the MISA fee sits outside it. Reproduce that construction rather than presenting a single all-in figure.

### Tax position — stated on the client slide, not the fee slide

**[SOURCED]** All three proposals carry the client's tax position as a muted callout on the *About the Client* slide rather than among the fees, which is the right place for it — it is not something Taajeel charges.

| Entity | As printed |
|---|---|
| Havenstone — foreign-owned LLC | *"Limited liability Company- Foreign"* · *"Subject to income tax provisions of **20% of net income**"* |
| HCP — foreign-owned LLC | same: *"Limited liability Company- Foreign"* · *"Subject to income tax provisions of 20% of net income"* |
| FlyAkeed — Saudi/GCC-owned | *"Limited liability Company- CCG"* · *"Subject to income tax provisions of **2.5% of amendment net income"*** |

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** The distinction is the familiar one: foreign-owned entities pay corporate **income tax**, Saudi and GCC-owned entities pay **zakat** at a much lower headline rate. A foreign founder will not know this and it materially affects their model, so it belongs in a proposal — as a statement of the applicable regime, not as advice. Note that Taajeel's own Terms disclaim responsibility for *"Zakat, or taxation matters"*, so the framing must stay descriptive. **[TO CONFIRM: whether the 20% figure is current and whether it applies to every foreign-owned structure.]**

### The standing notes, verbatim, on every fee slide

> - Government Fees are not included in Taajeel fees
> - Payments paid within five days of the maturity date
> - Payments to be made are non-refundable and payable upon signing the contract
> - We will issue the invoices based on the maturity date payments as described in the above method of payment
> - VAT will be charged (15%) of the total fees paid by the Client to obtain the Value Added Tax (VAT) issued by Zakat, Tax, and Customs Authority "ZATCA"

**The first note answers Q17's markup question structurally rather than by assertion.** Government fees are excluded from Taajeel's fee, so there is nothing to mark up. This is stronger than the claim "we do not mark them up" — which no source states and which `voice.md` specifically forbids inventing.

**VAT is charged at 15% and shown as its own figure**, not folded into the headline. That closes Q19 for Taajeel's practice, though whether a non-resident client is charged Saudi VAT on a place-of-supply basis is still unstated.

## Government fee figures cited on the Start Saudi blog

**⚠️ Read this framing before using any number below.**

The blog post *How Much Does It Cost to Register a Company in Saudi Arabia as a Foreigner?* cites third-party figures. It does **not** present them as Taajeel's prices, and — importantly — **the post's own argument is that published cost guides contradict each other**. It notes that some 2026 guides report the MISA fee as waived entirely while others cite thousands of riyals, and observes **[VERBATIM — blog]** that *"Saudi Arabia has used fee waivers as a temporary incentive before"*, leaving current status uncertain.

So these are **[SOURCED as cited by Start Saudi, NOT verified by this kit, and NOT Start Saudi's prices]**:

| Item | Figure as cited | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| MISA investment licence | Reported as waived in some 2026 guides; elsewhere SAR 2,000 upfront plus annual renewal, rising to SAR 60,000–62,000 depending on licence type | **Low — actively contradictory** |
| Trade name reservation | SAR 100–500 | Moderate |
| Commercial registration | ~SAR 1,200 | Moderate |
| E-publishing fee | ~SAR 500 | Moderate |
| Certified translation | ~SAR 100–300 per page | Moderate |
| Notarisation | ~SAR 100–200 per document | Moderate |
| National address registration | ~SAR 200 per year | Moderate |
| Government platform fees | SAR 1,000–4,000 per year | Moderate |
| Chamber of Commerce subscription | Annual, amount not given | — |

### Capital requirements, also as cited

| Structure | Figure as cited |
|---|---|
| General LLC | **No fixed minimum.** Capital must be *"sufficient to achieve the company's objective"* **[VERBATIM — blog]** |
| Joint Stock Company | SAR 500,000 (statutory) |
| Services LLC | ~SAR 500,000 (described as a practical benchmark, not a statute) |
| Industrial LLC | ~SAR 1,000,000+ (practical benchmark) |
| Retail / wholesale trading, 100% foreign-owned | **SAR 30,000,000** |

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** That last line is the one that changes deals. A foreign SME planning to sell goods at retail or wholesale with full foreign ownership faces a capital requirement roughly **sixty times** the practical benchmark cited for a services company. It is the most consequential single number in this file and it must be surfaced early in any proposal where trading activity is in scope — burying it would be precisely the "you don't know what you don't know" failure the brand exists to prevent.

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** Note also the distinction the blog draws between **statutory** minimums and **practical benchmarks**: post-2023 reform, a general LLC has no fixed minimum in law, but MISA in practice expects capital proportionate to the activity. A proposal should reproduce that distinction rather than quoting a single number as law.

### How to handle these in a proposal

- **Never present them as Start Saudi's fees or as a quotation.** They are third-party estimates cited in a blog post whose thesis is that such estimates disagree.
- **Prefer the structure to the numbers.** "Government fees are set by the authorities and vary by activity and company type" is accurate, on-brand and cannot go stale.
- **If a client insists on indicative figures**, cite them as ranges, attribute them as published third-party guidance, date them, and state that the confirmed figure comes with the written quotation. Handle the MISA licence fee separately and say plainly that sources currently conflict — that candour *is* the brand.
- **The capital requirement is not a fee.** It is money the client puts into their own company, not money paid to anyone. Clients confuse the two constantly and a proposal should separate them explicitly.

## What a proposal's commercial section should look like today

Until Q17–Q19 are answered, the honest and on-brand construction is:

> **What this costs**
>
> The price has two parts.
>
> **Taajeel's service fee** — for running the filings end to end: `[TO CONFIRM: service fee for this scope]`. VAT at 15% is charged on it and shown separately.
>
> **Government fees** — set by the authorities, varying by activity and company type, and **not included in the service fee**. For your activity and structure they come to `[TO CONFIRM: government fee total]`, excluding the MISA licence fee, which is confirmed in writing when it is billed.
>
> Payment: 50% on signing, 35% on approval of the MISA licence, 15% on issue of the commercial registration.
>
> You get both parts in writing before you commit, itemised. The discovery call is free.
>
> Separately, and not a fee: your company's share capital is money you put into your own company, not money paid to us or to the government. For `[activity]` the working expectation is `[TO CONFIRM: capital for this activity]`.

The milestone structure, the VAT treatment, the government-fee exclusion and the refusal to quote the MISA fee are all now **sourced from Taajeel's own proposals** rather than invented — see the section above. Only the numbers themselves remain open.

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** A generated proposal that fills those brackets with a plausible invented figure is the worst failure mode the whole system has. It is unrecoverable with a client, and it is specifically forbidden by the voice: a company whose pitch is *"you don't know what you don't know"* cannot be the company that quietly guesses at a price. **A visible `[TO CONFIRM]` is a correct output, not a defect.**
