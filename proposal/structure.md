# Proposal structure

Derived from three real Taajeel proposals, then adapted for Start Saudi.

---

## The evidence

| Proposal | Date | Client | Assignment | Format |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **HAVENSTONE Consulting W.L.L** | 12 Jun 2026 | Bahraini management consultancy, British + Brazilian owners | **Foreign company formation in Saudi Arabia** + GM resident visa | 33-slide PPTX, 16:9 |
| **HCP Architecture & Engineering Arabia** | 15 Jul 2026 | Spanish architecture firm, already MISA-licensed | HRO/GRO retainer + National Address & office services | 27-slide deck, PDF |
| **Akeed for Travel and Tourism (FlyAkeed)** | 11 Aug 2026 | Saudi travel-tech, UAE parent | Annual Confirmation + AoA amendment | 28-slide deck, PDF, DocuSigned |

**Havenstone is the template.** It is a foreign-owned company formation for an overseas SME expanding into the Kingdom — the same product Start Saudi sells, to the same kind of buyer. The other two calibrate what is fixed across engagements and what flexes.

### The first and most important finding

**Taajeel proposals are slide decks, not documents.** All three are 16:9 landscape, 27–33 slides, with section divider slides, infographic process diagrams and a signature page. Two were exported to PDF; one went out through DocuSign as an engagement letter.

This is a structural decision for whatever gets built on this kit. A Taajeel-shaped proposal is a **deck**. If Start Saudi's output is a document instead, that is a deliberate departure and should be a conscious one — see "The Start Saudi adaptation" below, which argues for keeping the deck.

## The canonical Taajeel skeleton

Section order is identical across all three. Two sections are conditional.

| # | Section | Havenstone | HCP | FlyAkeed | Slides |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cover** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | 1 |
| 2 | **Contents** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | 1 |
| 3 | **Introduction** — the letter | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | 2 (divider + letter) |
| 4 | **About Taajeel** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | 4 (divider + tagline + logo story + mission/values) |
| 5 | **About the Client** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | 2–3 |
| 6 | **Scope of work** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | 2–3 |
| 7 | **Time Frame** | ✓ | — | ✓ | 1–2 |
| 8 | **Scope of work (Additional and Optional)** | ✓ | — | ✓ | 1–3 |
| 8b | **Non-covered by the scope of work** | — | ✓ | — | 2 |
| 9 | **The Fees** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | 1–2 |
| 10 | **Signoff** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | 2 |
| 11 | **Terms and Conditions** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | 5 |
| 12 | **Back cover** — entity details | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | 1 |

**Sections 7 and 8 are conditional.** A one-off project gets a Time Frame; a monthly retainer (HCP) does not. Sections 8 and 8b are the same idea inverted — *here is what you can add* for a project, *here is what is excluded* for a retainer. Havenstone and FlyAkeed use "Additional and Optional"; HCP uses "Non-covered by the scope of work", and carries the note that non-covered items are done **"with additional fees that will be discussed with you upon request (case by case)"**.

Everything else is fixed. **Sections 3, 4, 10, 11 and 12 are boilerplate and reused verbatim**; the substance lives in 5, 6, 7 and 9.

### Slide-level anatomy of each section

**Cover.** Navy field, a curved swoosh cutout carrying a Riyadh skyline photograph. Client name and client logo lower-left; Taajeel logo right, in cream. Proposal title bottom-left in a muted blue-grey: *"Proposal for Professional Services of Foreign Company Formation in Saudi Arabia"*.

**Contents.** Two columns, "Title" and "Page", with the section names and slide numbers. Plain.

**Introduction.** A section divider, then a formal letter slide carrying, in this order: a reference number, the client's legal name, the client's full postal address, the date, `Atte:` a named individual, their mobile, telephone, email and sometimes website; then `Subject:` restating the assignment; then the body. The body is two sentences and it is near-identical in all three:

> "We Would Like To Thank You For Your Trust In Taajeel | تعجيل to Propose Professional Services in [assignment] ("Mission"/ "Assignment"). Accordingly, attached to Your Excellency in the Proposal of this scope of work, Project Timeline."
>
> "Truly yours,,,
> Taajeel Business Solutions LLC"

**[EXTRAPOLATED]** The reference numbers are `0026.04.06.0622` (HCP), `0026.07.12.0633` (Havenstone), `0026.08.11.0640` (FlyAkeed). The last four digits run in sequence with date, so the format appears to be `YYYY.MM.DD.NNNN` with a global proposal counter — the counter is consistent, though the date portion does not always match the letter date.

**About Taajeel.** Four slides, wholly boilerplate: divider; the bilingual tagline *"We accelerate your steps to build your company,,, / نعجل خطوتك، لتبني منظومتك،،،"*; the logo-symbolism slide (the Book, the Peregrine Falcon *صقر الشاهين*, Open Hands); and mission / vision / three strategy pillars (Focus, Innovation, Specialization) / six core values (Quality, Innovation, Integrity, Confidentiality, Partnership, Social Responsibility) / "Taajeel adding Value".

**About the Client.** The most substantive non-scope slide, and the best thing in the whole format. It states, in bold, the facts that determine the engagement: legal name, jurisdiction, registration number, incorporation date, paid-up capital, registered activity, named shareholders with nationalities and exact percentages, directors and signatories, then the strategic reason for entering Saudi Arabia, then the assignment. Havenstone then adds a sub-block, **"The Investment requirements"**, giving the licensing conditions and the exact document list the client must produce.

A right-hand rail carries the client's logo, jurisdiction flags (Bahrain → Saudi), the relevant ministry logo, and two muted callouts: the entity type (*"Limited liability Company- Foreign"*) and the tax position (*"Subject to income tax provisions of 20% of net income"*; FlyAkeed, as a Saudi/GCC-owned entity, instead reads *"2.5% of amendment net income"*).

**Scope of work.** A numbered journey rendered as an infographic — interlocking gears (Havenstone), a circular flow (FlyAkeed), a hub-and-spoke (HCP) — with a step count badge. Havenstone runs **two** paths on two slides: "The Journey of Setup a Foreign Limited Liability Company", **16 steps**; and "Issue the resident visa the main General Manager Path", **15 steps**.

**Time Frame.** A horizontal arrow with alternating above/below labels, a duration on each arc. Havenstone gives 16 nodes with 15 durations for the company path and a second slide for the visa path.

**The Fees.** A single dense slide: headline fee, VAT at 15% shown as its own figure, the payment-milestone list, then a numbered grid of optional services each with a price and a unit. Five standing notes at the foot. Havenstone adds a **separate Government fees slide** with each authority's logo beside its fee and an explicit "estimated" caveat.

**Signoff.** *"This document constitutes an 'Engagement Letter' for the Assignment presented, thus, kindly sign off and affix your official stamp."* Then two signature blocks side by side — Taajeel Business Solutions Co LLC / **Muath Abdullah A AlZahrani, General Manager** on the left, the client's blank block on the right.

**Terms and Conditions.** Five slides, nine clauses, verbatim identical across all three proposals: Definitions · Role of Taajeel · Authority · Information · Confidentiality · Taajeel Liability · Advertisements · Termination · Notices.

**Back cover.** Taajeel Business Solutions Co. LLC · CR: 1010941670 · Tel +966 92000 6247 · Mob +966 56 920 7725 · Riyadh, RHTA 7697, 7697 Abi Baker Siddiq AlTaawun, First floor, office No. 03 & 04 · www.taajeel.sa · ©Taajeel Proposal 2025/2026.

## What the proposals revealed that is a problem

### 1. The voice clash

**This is the central tension the proposal system has to resolve.**

Taajeel's "About Taajeel" slide reads, verbatim:

> "Taajeel stands as a beacon of excellence in the business solutions landscape, seamlessly merging innovation with expertise to empower organizations to reach their full potential. Rooted in a commitment to unparalleled quality, we provide comprehensive management consulting, administrative optimization, and strategic insights that drive success."

And closes:

> "By choosing Taajeel, you are partnering with a leader dedicated to delivering results-driven solutions that align with your goals, backed by a proven track record of success and a commitment to innovation and excellence."

Set that beside `content/voice.md`, which was written from the Start Saudi site before any of these documents existed. Its banned-words list contains *beacon*, *seamlessly*, *empower*, *solutions* as a noun, *comprehensive*, *results-driven*, *leader*, *proven track record*, *commitment to excellence*. Its "Before" examples — the generic consultancy prose being rewritten *away from* — are almost word-for-word the parent company's actual copy.

**The two brands write in opposite registers, and the difference is not stylistic.** Start Saudi's entire positioning is *"You don't know what you don't know"* — the claim that the market's problem is opacity and that Start Saudi's answer is to show you the path, including the bad parts. A proposal that opens with "a beacon of excellence" has broken that promise on slide 8.

**The resolution: keep Taajeel's structure, replace Taajeel's prose.** The skeleton is sound — it is a real engagement letter that real clients have signed, and its sequence (who you are → what you need → what we do → how long → what it costs → sign here) is correct. What must not survive is the About Taajeel language. See the adaptation below.

### 2. The timeline clash — the most important thing in this file

Taajeel's own Time Frame slide for the Havenstone foreign company formation:

**Company path — 16 milestones, 15 durations between them:** Workshop 1 · Apply for MISA licence 5 · Submit AoA to SBC 5 · Get approval 2 · Notarise AoA 1 · Pay government fees 1 · Advertise AoA 1 · Receive Commercial Register 3 · Ratify partners' signatures 3 · ZATCA 1 · National Address 1 · HRSD 1 · QIWA 1 · GOSI 2 · MUDAD 3 *(Muqeem is the sixteenth milestone and carries no duration of its own)*.

**Total: 31 days, presented sequentially with no parallelism shown.**

**GM resident visa path:** Submit support letter 2 · MISA approval 2 · HRSD procedures 3 · HRSD approval 1 · Work visa issuance 15 · Manpower office delegation 1 · Contract signing 1 · Resident visa issuance 15 · Employee arrival 3 · Ministry of Interior fees 1 · Work permit from QIWA 3. *(Three milestones on the chart — medical test, medical insurance and receipt of the Iqama — carry no duration.)*

**Total: a further 47 days.**

Start Saudi's public promise is **15 working days** — about 21 calendar days — for a path it describes in **6 steps**, with the MISA licence and trade name explicitly running in parallel.

These do not reconcile on their face. **[TO CONFIRM — this is now the single highest-priority open question in the kit, logged as Q35.]** The possibilities:

- Start Saudi is a genuinely different, leaner product — a standardised fast track where Havenstone was a bespoke engagement with a Bahraini corporate shareholder structure.
- Taajeel's chart is conservative, quotes calendar days, and shows no parallelism because the infographic cannot express it.
- The two are measuring different spans — exactly the failure Start Saudi's own blog warns about: *"check which span is actually being quoted rather than assuming the numbers describe the same thing."*

All three may be partly true. **What cannot happen is a Start Saudi proposal promising 15 working days for work the parent company's own client-facing timeline puts at 31 days.** Until Q35 is answered, a Start Saudi proposal should quote the 15-working-day promise **with its published conditions attached** — from complete documents, registration only, GM visa and bank account tracked separately — and should not present a day-by-day breakdown that contradicts Taajeel's.

### 3. The step-count clash

Taajeel shows 16 steps; Start Saudi shows 6. **[EXTRAPOLATED]** They are describing the same work at different granularity — Taajeel's 16 expands Start Saudi's steps 3–5 into their constituent filings and adds MUDAD and Muqeem, which the Start Saudi site never mentions. Neither is wrong. But **the Taajeel 16-step list is a useful annex** for a client who wants to see the detail, and `content/04-process.md`'s 6-step table is the right thing on the main slide.

## The Start Saudi adaptation

**Keep the deck.** Three real signed-or-sent proposals in this format is the strongest evidence available about what this business's clients expect, and a deck suits the content — the process is inherently a diagram, and the fee slide is inherently a grid. Build a 16:9 deck.

**Keep the section order.** It is a correct sales sequence and it doubles as an engagement letter.

**Keep the Terms and Conditions verbatim.** They are legal boilerplate that has gone out under signature three times. They are not a writing opportunity. One change is needed: they refer throughout to "Taajeel" as the contracting party, and **[TO CONFIRM: whether a Start Saudi proposal contracts as Taajeel Business Solutions Co. LLC or as a separate entity — Q8]**. Until that is answered, contract as Taajeel and say so plainly.

**Keep the About the Client slide, and make it better.** It is the best thing in the format: it proves the reader has been listened to before a single claim is made about the seller. For Start Saudi it should also carry the **activity band** (`content/07-client-types.md`) and the **attestation route** (`content/02-services/00-document-preparation.md`), because those two facts determine everything downstream and naming them on the client slide is the most on-brand move available.

### The changes

| Taajeel section | Start Saudi | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **About Taajeel** — 4 slides of mission, vision, values, logo symbolism | **Cut to 2 slides.** One: what Start Saudi does and the Taajeel endorsement, carrying the track-record numbers (4,638+ companies, SAR 5.1B+, 407,000+ services) attributed to Taajeel. Two: *"A founder who was where you are"* — the ChiefNest case study. | The mission/vision/values slide is the voice clash in its purest form, and it contains no information. The track record and the case study are the actual proof. |
| Logo symbolism slide | **Cut entirely.** | The Book, the Falcon and the Open Hands are Taajeel's identity story. Start Saudi's mark is a different mark and a proposal is not the place to explain either. |
| — | **Add: "You don't know what you don't know."** One slide, early — after About the Client, before Scope. The founder's own anxieties, then the whole path laid out as the answer. | This is the brand's strategic centre and the format currently has no slide for it. It is what makes the scope section land as reassurance rather than as a list. |
| **Scope of work** — 16-step gear diagram | **The 6-step path from `content/04-process.md`,** with durations, the parallelism shown, and the footnote *"The durations above are the government's, not ours."* The 16-step Taajeel list becomes an optional detail annex. | 6 steps is comprehensible; 16 is a wall. The footnote is non-negotiable. |
| **Time Frame** | **Keep, but as calendar dates in the client's own reckoning**, and with the pre-clock attestation phase drawn *before* day zero. | The single most useful thing a proposal can do for a foreign founder. See Q35 before drawing durations. |
| **Additional and Optional** | **Keep, and rename what it contains.** The bank account and the GM visa are not upsells in Start Saudi's telling — they are *"tracked separately"*, which is a scope boundary. Put them here, priced, but framed as the carve-out the homepage already makes. | Presenting the bank account as an optional extra contradicts the public promise; presenting it as a named, priced, separately-tracked step honours it. |
| **The Fees** | **Keep the structure exactly** — headline fee, VAT as its own figure, milestones, optional grid with units, standing notes, separate government-fee slide. | This is the best fee presentation in the three decks and it already does what `content/05-pricing.md` says the brand does: separates the two parts and attributes the part that is not theirs. |
| **Non-covered by the scope of work** | **Add it back, for project work too.** HCP has it; Havenstone does not. | A brand whose signature move is naming the boundary of its own promise should always carry the exclusions slide. |
| **Signoff, Terms, Back cover** | **Unchanged.** | Boilerplate that works. |

## Length and tone targets

- **Body copy: 8–18 words a sentence**, per `voice.md`. Taajeel's About slides run 30–40; that is the thing being fixed.
- **One idea per slide.** Taajeel's fee slide carries twelve prices and is still legible because it is a grid with units. Its About slide carries mission, vision, three pillars and six values, and is not.
- **Every abstraction itemised within one sentence.**
- **Every promise followed by its boundary**, in the same register, without apology.
- **British spelling** — "licence", "notarisation". Taajeel's decks are inconsistent on this; Start Saudi's site is not.
