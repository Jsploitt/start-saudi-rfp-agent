# Section briefs

One brief per slide of the Start Saudi deck defined in `structure.md`. Each gives **the question the slide answers**, **the evidence it needs**, and **the failure mode to avoid** — the failure modes are the useful part, because most of them are things the Taajeel decks actually do.

A writer or a model should be able to work from one brief plus the named `content/` file and produce the slide.

---

## 1 · Cover

**Question it answers:** Is this for me, and from whom?

**Evidence:** Client's exact legal name. Assignment in one line, in the client's own terms. Start Saudi horizontal lockup (`brand/assets/mark-color.png` on light, `mark-light.png` on dark — *not* the misnamed "horizontal" files). Taajeel endorsement, secondary. Date.

**Failure mode:** Blending the two brands' visual systems. Taajeel's covers are parchment-and-navy with a Riyadh skyline in a swoosh cutout; Start Saudi is navy-and-green with a gradient mark and a two-tone pattern. Pick Start Saudi's, put Taajeel's mark on it as an endorsement, and do not composite the two design languages. Second failure: a generic title. *"Proposal for Professional Services"* is what Taajeel writes; *"Your Saudi company, registered in 15 working days"* is what this brand writes.

---

## 2 · Contents

**Question:** How long is this and what is in it?

**Evidence:** Section names and slide numbers, two columns, exactly as all three Taajeel decks do it.

**Failure mode:** Renaming sections to sound impressive. "Commercial Framework" instead of "The Fees". Taajeel writes "The Fees" and it is the better word.

---

## 3 · Introduction — the letter

**Question:** Who is this addressed to, about what, and on what date?

**Evidence:** Reference number (`YYYY.MM.DD.NNNN` — see `structure.md`). Client's legal name and full registered address. Date. `Atte:` a named individual with their mobile and email, taken from the RFP. `Subject:` restating the assignment, naming it as the "Mission"/"Assignment" so the Terms have an antecedent.

**Failure mode:** Keeping Taajeel's body wording. *"Attached to Your Excellency in the Proposal of this scope of work"* is not English, and *"We Would Like To Thank You For Your Trust"* in title case is not this brand. Write two plain sentences that do the same job: acknowledge the enquiry, say what is attached. Keep the formality of the letter furniture — reference number, address block, subject line — because that is what makes the document an engagement letter rather than a brochure.

---

## 4 · Start Saudi, powered by Taajeel

**Question:** Who are you, and why should I believe you can do this?

**Evidence:** `content/01-company.md` for the relationship; `content/03-credentials.md` for the numbers. The four figures — 4,638+ companies, SAR 5.1B+ capital, 407,000+ services, 15 working days — **always attributed to Taajeel**, `+` signs kept, never rounded. A line of recognisable client names in text (PKF Global, Grand View Research, OpenSooq, Ajlan & Bros Holding) rather than logos, until logo permission is confirmed.

**Failure mode:** **This is where the voice clash bites, and it is the highest-risk slide in the deck.** Taajeel's equivalent opens *"Taajeel stands as a beacon of excellence in the business solutions landscape, seamlessly merging innovation with expertise to empower organizations…"*. Every content word in that sentence is on the banned list in `voice.md`. A model that has seen the Taajeel decks will reproduce that register unless stopped. The slide should contain **no adjectives about Taajeel at all** — just what Start Saudi does, what Taajeel is, and four numbers.

Second failure: claiming the numbers as Start Saudi's. Third: presenting them as verified — they are self-published and uncorroborated (`03-credentials.md`).

## 5 · A founder who was where you are

**Question:** Has someone like me done this?

**Evidence:** `content/03-credentials.md`. Dr. Sulaiman Alsalameh, founder of ChiefNest, United States. The two quotes: *"You don't know what you don't know. And that feeling sucks."* and *"I went to a few places first and lost money."* And the disclosure, in the same breath: *"Sulaiman is a Taajeel client and a partner in Start Saudi."*

**Failure mode:** Dropping the disclosure. The public homepage carries it; a proposal that omits it is concealing something the website discloses, which is both dishonest and pointless. Second failure: putting this in a testimonial box at the end. It belongs early — it is the bridge from "who we are" to "here is what you're feeling".

**Note:** the case study still has no dates or outcome (`OPEN-QUESTIONS` Q13). Until it does, this slide is two quotes and a disclosure, which is honest but thin.

## 6 · About the Client

**Question:** Do you actually understand my situation?

**Evidence:** The best slide in the Taajeel format — copy its discipline. Legal name, jurisdiction, registration number, incorporation date, paid-up capital, registered activity, named shareholders with nationalities and exact percentages, directors and signatories. Then the strategic reason for entering Saudi Arabia, in the client's own framing. Then the assignment.

Start Saudi should add two things Taajeel's version does not carry:

- **The activity band** — open / restricted / capital-gated / closed, per `content/07-client-types.md`. State it explicitly: *"Your activity falls in the open band; 100% foreign ownership applies."*
- **The attestation route** — apostille or embassy chain, per `content/02-services/00-document-preparation.md`, derived from the client's jurisdiction.

Also carry Taajeel's right-rail callouts: entity type (*"Limited liability Company- Foreign"*) and tax position (*"Subject to income tax provisions of 20% of net income"*).

**Failure mode:** Getting a fact wrong here destroys the rest of the document — this is the slide that proves you listened. Every fact must come from the RFP or from the client's public register, and anything uncertain must be marked rather than assumed. Second failure: stating the activity band confidently when the RFP does not give enough detail to classify. *"On what you have told us, this is an open-band activity; we confirm the classification before you commit"* is the brand's own construction.

## 7 · You don't know what you don't know

**Question:** Do you understand what I'm actually worried about?

**Evidence:** `content/01-company.md` and `voice.md`. The homepage copy: *"Am I missing a deadline? Is there a payment I was supposed to make? Is something up for renewal that nobody told me about?"* and *"Most founders entering Saudi Arabia can see the opportunity clearly and cannot see the path at all. That gap is the whole problem, and it is expensive."*

**Failure mode:** Two opposite ones. Cutting it, because it looks like it contains no information — it is the strategic centre of the brand and it is what makes the scope slide land as relief rather than as a price justification. Or over-writing it into emotional marketing — the site earns this register by rationing it to one sentence and then immediately showing a table of durations. One slide, then straight into the path.

---

## 8 · Scope of work — the whole path

**Question:** What exactly will you do?

**Evidence:** The six-step table from `content/04-process.md` — MISA licence 10 working days, trade name 48 hours in parallel, articles and notarisation 72 hours, commercial registration 24 hours, government accounts 24 hours, then "after incorporation". Each step's one-line description is already written and quotable. Detail per step from `content/02-services/01`–`05`.

**The durations belong here.** They are Start Saudi's own published figures, they appear on its homepage, and a scope slide without them would be weaker than the website. The prohibition in § 9 is narrower than it looks: it is about **not drawing a second, competing day-by-day chart** on the Time Frame slide, not about suppressing the published six-step figures.

**Failure mode:** **Omitting the footnote.** *"Steps 1 and 2 run in parallel. The durations above are the government's, not ours."* It is not a disclaimer to be tidied away into small print; it is the line that makes the fifteen days credible. Second failure: reproducing Taajeel's 16-step gear diagram because it looks more thorough. Sixteen interlocking gears is a wall; six steps with durations is a path. The 16-step list is an annex for a client who asks. Third: not explaining the parallelism, so the reader adds 2 + 10 and concludes the arithmetic is a trick.

## 9 · Time Frame

**Question:** When will I actually have a company?

**Evidence:** `content/04-process.md`. Working days converted to **calendar dates in the client's own week** — Saudi working days are Sunday to Thursday, which a European or American client's planning will not assume. The attestation phase drawn **before day zero**, on the client's side of the line. The post-CR steps drawn after.

**Failure mode:** **The biggest one in the deck.** Taajeel's own Time Frame for a comparable foreign company formation totals 31 days for the company and a further 47 for the GM visa (see `structure.md`, and `OPEN-QUESTIONS` Q35). Start Saudi promises 15 working days.

**The rule, precisely.** Start Saudi's six published step durations stay on the **scope** slide (§ 8) — they are the brand's own figures and it would be odd to hide them. What must **not** happen until Q35 is answered is a **Time Frame slide that lays out its own competing day-by-day sequence**, because any such sequence is either Taajeel's 31-day one (contradicting the 15-day promise) or a new one invented to fit (contradicting Taajeel). Give the client **calendar dates and milestones** instead — documents complete by X, CR by Y, bank by Z — which answers the question they actually asked without asserting per-step durations that the two sources disagree about.

Second failure: starting the clock at signature. It starts when documents are complete, and saying so is what the brand does.

## 10 · Tracked separately — bank account and GM visa

**Question:** When can I actually trade, hire and get paid?

**Evidence:** `content/02-services/06-bank-account-opening.md` and `07-gm-visa-and-residency.md`. The bank: 2–3 weeks after an active CR, run by the bank, may require a visit, parent documents needed again. The GM visa: the own-versus-reside decision, and Taajeel's documented 15-step path.

**Failure mode:** Presenting these as upsells. The homepage says *"GM visa and bank account, tracked separately"* — they are a scope boundary that happens to be purchasable, and the framing must be the boundary first, the price second. Second failure: quietly folding them into the headline timeline to look faster. Third: promising a bank outcome. The bank decides.

## 11 · Not covered

**Question:** What will I get billed for later?

**Evidence:** `content/05-pricing.md` and the service files. HCP's deck has this slide and it is a good one — it lists the excluded items explicitly and says they are available *"with additional fees that will be discussed with you upon request (case by case)"*.

**Failure mode:** Omitting it because Havenstone did. A brand whose signature move is naming the boundary of its own promise should always carry the exclusions slide. Second failure: writing it defensively, as liability-shifting. The register is *"here is what is not in the price, and here is what it would cost"* — not *"delays caused by the client are excluded"*.

## 12 · The Fees

**Question:** What does this cost, and what am I paying for?

**Evidence:** `content/05-pricing.md` for the model, and the Havenstone fee slide for the format. Two parts, named and attributed. VAT at 15% shown as its own figure, not folded in. Payment milestones. Optional services in a numbered grid, each with a **unit** — per bank, per document, per licence, monthly, for every 250 words.

**Failure mode:** **Inventing a price.** The Havenstone quote — SAR 40,000 for foreign company setup plus GM resident visa — is *one quote, to one client, for one scope, in June 2026*. It is a calibration point, not a price list, and it is not Start Saudi's price. Until Q17 is answered for Start Saudi's own product, this slide carries `[TO CONFIRM]`. A visible bracket is a correct output; a plausible invented figure is the worst failure the system can produce, and it is unrecoverable with a client.

Second failure: adjectives. "Competitive", "transparent", "excellent value" are all banned. The transparency is performed by splitting the fee, not asserted.

Third: dropping the standing notes. Payments within five days of maturity; non-refundable and payable on signing; invoices issued on the maturity dates; VAT at 15%; **government fees not included**. That last one answers the markup question structurally and should never be cut.

---

## 13 · Government fees

**Question:** What do I pay that isn't your fee?

**Evidence:** `content/05-pricing.md`, and Havenstone's separate government-fee slide as the model — each authority named, its fee beside it, the total marked **estimated**, and anything genuinely unknown shown as unknown. Havenstone prints `#,###` with *"Yet to be determined once billed will share with client"* against the MISA fee, which is exactly the right move and exactly what `voice.md` argues for.

**Failure mode:** Quoting the MISA licence fee. Start Saudi's own blog says published sources contradict each other on it, and Taajeel's own proposal declines to state it. Follow both. Second failure: presenting the Havenstone government figures as current or as applicable to a different client — they are one engagement's estimate. Third: confusing **capital** with **fees**. Share capital is money the client puts into their own company; a proposal that lists it among the costs is misleading, and `05-pricing.md` insists on the distinction.

## 14 · Signoff

**Question:** How do I say yes?

**Evidence:** Taajeel's wording, which works: *"This document constitutes an 'Engagement Letter' for the Assignment presented, thus, kindly sign off and affix your official stamp."* Two signature blocks. Taajeel Business Solutions Co LLC / Muath Abdullah A AlZahrani, General Manager on the left; the client's, blank, on the right.

**Failure mode:** Naming the wrong contracting party. **[TO CONFIRM: whether Start Saudi proposals contract as Taajeel Business Solutions Co. LLC or as a separate entity — Q8.]** Until answered, contract as Taajeel and say so. Second failure: adding a marketing close. The signature page is not a place for one more pitch.

## 15 · Terms and Conditions

**Question:** What am I agreeing to?

**Evidence:** Taajeel's nine clauses, verbatim: Definitions · Role of Taajeel · Authority · Information · Confidentiality · Taajeel Liability · Advertisements · Termination · Notices. Identical across all three proposals. Liability capped at fees actually paid. Arbitration at the GCC Commercial Arbitration Centre. One-year term, auto-renewing, 90 days' notice; 14 days to remedy a material breach.

**Failure mode:** Rewriting them. This is legal boilerplate that has gone out under signature three times; it is not a writing opportunity and `voice.md` does not apply to it. Second failure: not noticing that they contradict the sales pitch in one place — the **Role of Taajeel** clause disclaims responsibility for *"legal, regulatory, accounting, Zakat, or taxation matters"*, which sits awkwardly beside a proposal that has just classified the client's activity band and advised on structure. Flag it rather than paper over it; see `OPEN-QUESTIONS` Q26 and Q34.

## Cross-cutting rules

Apply to every slide.

1. **A number wherever a number exists.** "Quickly" fails if a figure is available.
2. **Every abstraction itemised within one sentence.**
3. **Every promise followed by its boundary**, in the same flat register, with no apology and no "unfortunately".
4. **`[TO CONFIRM: …]` rather than a plausible guess.** A company whose pitch is *"you don't know what you don't know"* cannot be the company that quietly fills a gap with an invented number.
5. **Nothing that could appear unchanged in a competitor's brochure.**
6. **British spelling**, and "Taajeel" attributed wherever a credential is claimed.
