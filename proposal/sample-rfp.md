# Sample RFP — demo input

A realistic inbound enquiry for a foreign SME / owner-operator, the segment chosen for this kit (`content/07-client-types.md` § 1). Its shape follows the Havenstone engagement: a small overseas company, two shareholders, expanding into Saudi Arabia on the back of a specific commercial opportunity.

**It contains deliberate ambiguities. They are the point of the exercise** — the system is meant to notice them and ask, not to resolve them silently. They are catalogued at the end of this file; read the RFP first without looking.

## The deliberate ambiguities

**Do not include this section in anything sent to a client.** It is the answer key.

A good proposal surfaces all four. A weak one answers the brief as written and inherits every one of them as a future dispute.

### Ambiguity 1 — the activity classification, and it is load-bearing

**The setup:** Meridian both *supplies equipment* (45%) and *services and calibrates* it (55%). The brief never asks which side of the line that falls, because Katherine does not know the line exists.

**Why it matters:** `content/07-client-types.md` and `content/05-pricing.md` — a services company in the open band has **no minimum capital fixed in law**, with a practical benchmark commonly cited around **SAR 500,000**. Retail and wholesale trading at 100% foreign ownership carries a cited requirement of **SAR 30,000,000** — roughly sixty times that. That is the difference between a viable expansion and an impossible one, and it turns entirely on how the activity is classified.

Note that `05-pricing.md` insists on the distinction between a **statutory minimum** and a **practical benchmark**, and a proposal that flattens the two is reproducing the error it is meant to catch.

**Intended resolution:** Raise it on the About the Client slide, before anything else. Say plainly that the classification determines the capital requirement, give the two outcomes and their consequences, and say it is confirmed before the client commits — which is the site's own promise: *"A small number of activities are restricted or need extra approvals, and we confirm yours before you commit."* Do **not** classify it confidently from the information given; the brief does not contain enough. Do **not** bury it in a caveats section.

This is the single highest-value thing a proposal can do for this client, and a proposal that misses it is negligent rather than merely incomplete.

### Ambiguity 2 — is the GM visa in scope?

**The setup:** Section 3 asks to employ engineers locally. Section 5 mentions Daniel Ayre relocating "at some point in year one" and never asks for anything. Neither shareholder wants to move; Katherine explicitly cannot.

**Why it matters:** `content/02-services/07-gm-visa-and-residency.md`. Someone must be the registered General Manager. The owners are not going to be resident. So either Daniel becomes the GM and needs the residency path, or a resident GM is appointed from outside the company, or a temporary GM is engaged — Taajeel prices exactly this at SAR 8,000 for three months, so it is a real and offered service. The GM visa is explicitly outside the 15-working-day promise and carries its own timeline, which Taajeel's own chart puts at roughly 47 days.

**Intended resolution:** Name the decision rather than making it. Lay out the three routes, say what each costs in time and money, note that the articles of association name the GM and that the decision is therefore needed by step 3 — not in year one. Put it in the "tracked separately" section, priced, and framed as a boundary rather than an upsell.

### Ambiguity 3 — the timeline conflicts with the launch date

**The setup:** The package begins **1 March 2027**. The approved-supplier listing must happen "before the end of January". Katherine assumes the company simply needs to exist by then. The RFP is dated 15 September 2026 and proposals are due 3 October.

**Why it matters:** `content/04-process.md`. *"Treat 'company registered' and 'company operating' as two different milestones."* Working backwards: the bank account needs 2–3 weeks after an **active** CR; the CR needs 15 working days from **complete documents**; complete documents need attestation, which sits outside the clock and which Katherine has explicitly never done; and she does not yet have her 2026 accounts, which are not filed until end of October.

Invoicing in riyals requires a bank account, not a CR. The end-of-January supplier listing probably requires only the CR — but that is an assumption about the client's process, not a fact, and the proposal should say so rather than guess.

**Intended resolution:** Work the date backwards in the proposal and show it. Identify attestation as the critical path and tell her to start it now, before choosing a provider. Distinguish what is needed for the January listing from what is needed to invoice on 1 March. Flag the accounts-filing dependency. This is the *"You don't know what you don't know"* slide doing actual work rather than being decorative.

### Ambiguity 4 — no budget, and no basis for one

**The setup:** Section 4 asks for "a complete breakdown of costs". No budget, no range, no indication of what she expects.

**Why it matters:** `content/05-pricing.md`. No Start Saudi price is published, and as of this kit the service fee for Start Saudi's own product is `[TO CONFIRM]` (Q17). The Havenstone quote — SAR 40,000 for a foreign company setup plus GM resident visa — is one quote to one client and is **not** a price list.

**Intended resolution:** Give the *structure* completely and honestly: two parts, government fees attributed and not marked up, VAT at 15% shown separately, milestone payments, optional items with units. Put `[TO CONFIRM]` where the number belongs. Separate **capital** from **fees** explicitly — Katherine will otherwise read a capital requirement as a cost. A visible bracket is the correct output here; an invented figure, to a reader who has already been burned by a Dubai adviser who took a deposit and went quiet, is unrecoverable.

### What else a good proposal notices

Not ambiguities, but tells:

- **"I'd rather have a straight answer than a polished one"** and **"I am slightly allergic to vagueness"** — she has described this brand's positioning back to it. The proposal should be plainer than usual, not more elaborate.
- **She found them through the ownership blog post.** She has already read the plain-English register and will notice if the proposal does not match it.
- **"Three of you I'm asking."** Competitive. `content/04-process.md` has the ready-made framing: *"check which span is actually being quoted rather than assuming the numbers describe the same thing."*
- **The Dubai adviser who took a deposit and went quiet.** Milestone payments tied to visible government outcomes — 50% on signing, 35% on MISA approval, 15% on CR — answer that anxiety directly, and should be presented as answering it.
- **Subsidiary vs branch**, on which she has invited a view. `content/02-services/08-branch-of-foreign-company.md` — the branch requires audited financials and can only do what the parent already does; her 2026 accounts are not filed until end of October, which is itself an argument.
- **Dr. Bekele's dual nationality** is almost certainly irrelevant to a MISA application but she may worry that it is not. One sentence closing it off is worth including.
